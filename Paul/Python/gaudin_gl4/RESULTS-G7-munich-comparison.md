## 3. D1: correctness (Munich vs ours)

**2a, multiplicity-free, vector by vector (`bash hwcgc.sh` -> `munichcmp`): 42 of 42 MATCH** (34 irreps of [S,S,0,0]^2, S = 1..4; plus S = 6: `0 0 0 0`, `2 1 1 0`, `6 4 2 0`, `12 12 0 0`; S = 8: `0 0 0 0`, `2 1 1 0`, `8 6 2 0`, `16 16 0 0`). Same support in every case; **max deviation after one global sign 1.4e-15** (acceptance 1e-9). Signs sigma are in the table of `munich_bridge_output.txt` (+/- both occur). Munich solve times of these runs (hwcgc.sh includes the full descendant table of the target): S = 8 singlet 18.9 s (RSS 9.3 GB, within the 10 GB guard), `2 1 1 0` 14.8 s, `8 6 2 0` 854 s, S = 6 `12 12 0 0` 130 s. **One exception to the method:** `hwcgc.sh` for S = 8 `16 16 0 0` (target dim 8721, full descendant table) was killed by `timeout 1800`; this one was compared with the repaired profiler's HW output instead (`HW_MODE=eig` prints the HW vector only; n_W = 1, one coefficient, MATCH, marked MATCH(prof)), so the original `clebsch` binary was NOT validated on that irrep.

**2b, multiplicity sectors, subspace by subspace: 100 sectors (sum m = 210, max m = 12), all OK.** For every sector the m Munich alpha-copies (Gram-Schmidt orthonormal, parsed from `hwcgc.sh ... alpha` for alpha = 1..m, or all at once from the profiler for [6,4,2,0]^2) span exactly the same subspace as our m canonical copies: worst max |1 - sigma_i(Q)| = 2.4e-15 for the 94 sectors run through the original `clebsch` (products [2,1,0,0]^2, [2,1,1,0]^2, [2,1,0,0]x[1,1,0,0], [1,1,0,0]x[2,1,0,0], [2,2,1,0]x[2,1,1,0], [3,2,1,0]^2, [4,2,0,0]^2, [4,2,1,0]^2: 7+6+4+4+6+16+24+27 irreps, sum m = 148, including (5,4,2,1) of [3,2,1,0]^2 with m = 4 where V3 is degenerate), and 1.6e-12 for the six sectors of [6,4,2,0]^2 with the largest multiplicities (m = 12, 11, 10, 10, 10, 9; (9,7,5,3), (10,7,5,2), (10,7,4,3), (9,8,5,2), (10,8,4,2), (10,6,5,3)); these used the repaired profiler's printed 12-decimal vectors (`cgc_hw_bench`/`prof` print the HW coefficients per alpha when not asked for `machine`), hence 1e-12. Munich's own orthonormality error: worst 5.6e-15 (original), 1.7e-12 (profiler, 12 decimals). Residual of the Munich vectors outside the span of ours: <= 3e-15 (original), 2.9e-11 (profiler). The brief's list gives 94 sectors, so its acceptance ">= 100 sectors, sum m >= 170" is met only with the six [6,4,2,0]^2 sectors (100 sectors, sum m = 210). **Q matrices** (rotation from our canonical (ev3, ev4a, ev4b)-ordered copies k, rows, to Munich's alpha copies, columns) are printed to 3 decimals in `munich_bridge_output.txt` for all 38 sectors with m >= 2: **none is a signed permutation** (`signed-perm=False` everywhere), i.e. Munich's Gram-Schmidt copies are genuinely mixtures of the Gaudin eigenvectors. Examples: m = 2 for (3,2,1,0) in [2,1,0,0]^2 (Munich label 3 2 1 0): Q = [[0.950, -0.312], [-0.312, -0.950]]; (3,3,2,1) in [2,2,1,0]x[2,1,1,0]: [[-0.328, -0.945], [-0.945, 0.328]]; m = 4 (5,4,2,1) in [3,2,1,0]^2: rows (-0.516, 0.033, -0.048, 0.854), (-0.338, 0.288, -0.856, -0.264), (-0.668, -0.624, 0.167, -0.370), and the fourth row in the output file. `munich_bridge.py selftest`: 29 of 29 outputs built from our vectors with a random rotation of the copies are accepted, 39 of 39 corrupted ones (perturbed component, component leaving the span) are rejected.

Reading: what is validated is that Munich's SU(4) HW Clebsch-Gordan coefficients (original `clebsch` / `cgc_hw_bench` code, GT basis with Q(M) = our GT index + 1, orthonormal GT basis = ours rescaled by 1/sqrt(g), trivial sign gauge) coincide with ours to 1e-15, vector by vector when m = 1, as subspaces when m >= 2. The extra information of ours (the canonical rotation inside the multiplicity space) is what the Q matrices record.

## 4. Surprises, limits, what was not run

* The support counts first printed in the comparison counted exact zeros of ours (e.g. 270 versus Munich's 65); corrected to |value| > 1e-9 (both now agree in every sector). No other discrepancy.
* `libopenblas-dev` is absent; `cgc_hw_prof_ob` links the env OpenBLAS by path. Note: a failed link deleted the previously existing `cgc_hw_prof_ob` binary; rebuilt.
* The eig variant is NOT always the best: at large n_W (>= 3000) on [6,4,2,0]^2 and [8,4,0,0]^2, one irrep with n_W = 4743 takes 13 s, and compact is faster on mid-size n_W but needs n_W x n_states memory and dgesvd (not run above n_W = 1500). The sums in section 1 mix run sets; the "common rows" paragraph gives equal-set numbers.
* Not run: S = 9 orig (33 GB); eig S = 12 (killed when Paul changed the priority; partial run discarded); `HW_MODE=compact` S >= 9; reps = 3 for compact; `proforig` for S = 5, 7; the originals on [6,4,2,0]^2, [9,6,3,0]^2 and on the irreps of [8,4,0,0]^2 with n_W dim^2 8 B > 10 GB (35 of 115); every Munich variant on the 47 irreps of [9,6,3,0]^2 with n_W > 6000; eig/compact beyond their n_W guards; the full `hwcgc.sh` on [6,4,2,0]^2 (only profiler output).
* Reps: July reference was reps = 1; "orig now" is reps = 1; "min of 3" is quoted separately; products use reps 5/3/1 by n_W.
