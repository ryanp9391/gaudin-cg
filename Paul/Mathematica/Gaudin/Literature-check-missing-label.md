## 0. Bottom line

The literature is much closer to our result than the brief assumed. Three papers dominate:

1. **Crampe, Poulain d'Andecy, Vinet, "The missing label of su_3 and its symmetry", Commun. Math. Phys. 400 (2023) 179-213, arXiv:2110.03521 [FT]** (with its companion **J. Lie Theory 31 (2021) 1085-1112, arXiv:2005.13444 [AB]**). They give, for the tensor product of two arbitrary su(3) irreps: the cubic missing-label operator X (a mixed polarised trace, two generators on one factor, one on the other), its matrix on the multiplicity space as an explicit **tridiagonal matrix with closed-form entries** (their eq. (18)), cyclicity and simple spectrum, the identification of X with the two-site rational Gaudin model, its diagonalisation by the **nested Bethe ansatz with the Mukhin-Varchenko polynomial ODEs**, and, in the conclusion, the suggestion that "the correlation functions of the Gaudin model should provide a way to compute the Clebsch-Gordan coefficients of su3" (our programme).
2. **Pluhar, Smirnov, Tolstoy, J. Phys. A 19 (1986) 21-28 [AB only]**: SU(3) CG coefficients defined through a labelling operator built from the generators, "an algebraic algorithm for computing the coefficients"; CPV state that X's matrix on the multiplicity space was already computed there (quasi-tridiagonal, eigenvalues distinct).
3. **Campoamor-Stursberg, Musso, J. Phys. A 46 (2013) 335201 [AB only]**: for the N = 2 rational Gaudin model based on sl(3), the spectral invariants of the Lax matrix are linear combinations of the labelling operators of sl(3) in sl(3)+sl(3).

I checked by hand (Appendix A) that **our V has the same spectrum as CPV's X_m up to an additive Casimir constant** in four sectors (three of [4,2,0]^2 with multiplicities 2, 2, 3, one of them with l != n, and the octet of 8 x 8). So V is the known operator; it is not new, and neither is "two-site Gaudin cubic Hamiltonian = missing-label operator". What remains new, as far as I could find: (i) the Gaudin-SoV/jet formula for CG slices in terms of Q-functions; (ii) the **GT-basis Jacobi chain with closed forms (eq. 8), the sub-chain/spectral-subspace description of M_mu, and the explicit statement slice_k = p_k(V)**, which is a different basis from CPV's and PST's; (iii) the slice null-space form of the PRV theorem as a numerical engine and its benchmark against the general-N null-space code.

## 1. What Crampe-Poulain d'Andecy-Vinet actually contain (read in full, `[FT]`)

Source: arXiv:2110.03521 (PDF and TeX source), CMP 400 (2023) 179-213, DOI 10.1007/s00220-022-04596-3. Notation: [m1,m2] = (Dynkin labels + 1) = (l1-l2+1, l2-l3+1); polarised traces T^(a1,...,ak) = e_{i2 i1}^(a1) e_{i3 i2}^(a2) ... e_{i1 ik}^(ak).

* **The operator.** X = (1/2)(T^(1,1,2) - T^(1,2,2)) + (1/3)(l1 - l2) = T^(1,1,2) + (1/6)(3 l1 - l2 - l3) + (3/4)(k3 - k2 - k1), where k_i, l_i are the quadratic and cubic Casimirs (of site 1, site 2, diagonal). So X = (mixed cubic invariant with two generators on site 1 and one on site 2) + Casimirs, which is our V up to terms that are constants on M_mu. The quartic Y is the second generator of the centraliser Z_2(su3); CPV remark that Y "does not appear in the context of integrable systems" (so the Gaudin choice is the cubic X). Generators of the centraliser: CPV21.
* **Explicit matrices (their eq. 18).** With the six parameters xi_1..xi_6 built from the three highest weights (two cases, l <= n and n <= l, l and n being the two triality combinations of the six labels), d = xi_a - xi_b = multiplicity, X_m is d x d tridiagonal with a_{j,j+1} = prod_{i=1..3}(j + xi_b - xi_i), a_{j+1,j} = prod_{i=4..6}(j + xi_b - xi_i), and explicit cubic diagonal a_jj. All off-diagonal entries are nonzero, so X_m is cyclic with simple spectrum; Y_m is tridiagonal in the same basis. **Note:** a_{j,j+1} a_{j+1,j} is always negative (three negative and three positive factors), so this chain is *not* a Jacobi matrix in the positive sense; reality of the spectrum is not manifest.
* **The basis.** Appendix A: the multiplicity space is identified with the highest-weight vectors; with the **extremal projector P of Asherova-Smirnov-Tolstoy**, M_m is spanned by omega_m = P (1 (x) e_32^m e_21^(n-1) e_32^(l-1-m) / factorials) v_0, v_0 = hw (x) hw' (and the mirror family omega'_m). Vanishing of omega_m outside max{l-m2', n-m1, 0} <= m < min{l, m2, m1'+l-n} comes from sl(2) string arguments. "The general idea is borrowed from [PST]". This is the dual (extremal-projector) form of the same theorem that underlies our slice map.
* **Spectrum.** "There does not seem to exist exact formulas, in the general case, for the eigenvalues." Section 1.6: X_m is the Heun-Hahn operator that "has been identified in the context of the two-body homogeneous rational Gaudin models in [Campoamor-Stursberg-Musso]"; the nested Bethe ansatz of Jurco (J. Math. Phys. 30 (1989) 1289) gives the eigenvalues in terms of Bethe roots (their eq. 31), and via Mukhin-Varchenko the Bethe roots are the zeros of polynomial solutions (degrees n-1 and l-1) of the coupled ODEs (32)-(33), "closely related to the Jacobi-Pineiro polynomials".
* **su(2) precedent.** Section 1.5: for su(2) CG coefficients, in the uncoupled basis H1 is diagonal and H2 (the Casimir of the diagonal algebra) is tridiagonal, with the Hahn recurrence coefficients as entries (Granovskii-Zhedanov 1988, `[CIT]`); X_m is a combination of Hahn matrices (a "Heun-Hahn operator", Prop. 1.4).
* **Conclusion of CPV (quoted):** "Firstly, the correlation functions of the Gaudin model should provide a way to compute the Clebsch-Gordan coefficients of su3."

## 2. C1: the slice reduction (PRV / Zhelobenko)

**Verdict: PARTIALLY KNOWN.** Theorem known and attributed; algorithmic uses exist in related forms; the specific null-space-on-the-slice engine and benchmark were not found. Confidence 70% (could not read Zhelobenko, PST, D-A, Braunschweig).

**Attribution of the statement.**
* S. Kumar, "Tensor product decomposition", Proc. ICM Hyderabad 2010, vol. IV (pages UNVERIFIED, about 1226-1261), `[FT]`: Theorem 3.7 (garbled in extraction but unambiguous): m^nu_{lambda,mu} = dim{v in V(mu)_{nu-lambda} : e_i^{<lambda,alpha_i^vee>+1} v = 0 for all simple alpha_i}, "due to Kostant [Ko, Lemma 4.1]", with [Ko] = B. Kostant, "A formula for the multiplicity of a weight", Trans. Amer. Math. Soc. 93 (1959) 53-73. Kumar attributes to PRV (Ann. of Math. 85 (1967) 383-429) the multiplicity-one of V(lambda + w_0 mu) (Cor. 1 to Thm 2.1) and the PRV conjecture that Kumar proved (Invent. Math. 93 (1988) 117-130, DOI 10.1007/bf01393689; independently Mathieu). So the statement of the brief (condition on the slice in V_Lambda[mu - Lambda']) is the Kostant/PRV lemma; the name "Zhelobenko" is attached by the Gelfand-Tsetlin / extremal-projector literature (book: D. P. Zhelobenko, *Compact Lie Groups and Their Representations*, AMS Transl. Math. Monographs 40, 1973). **I could not determine which theorem of the book states it** (UNVERIFIED), nor Zhelobenko's 1962 Russian Math. Surveys 17 (1962) 1-94 (`[MEM]`). A secondary statement is in arXiv:1908.08695 (tensor hierarchy algebras, "PRV states") `[CIT]`.

**Algorithmic uses, related but not identical:**
* *Extremal projector method.* R. M. Asherova, Yu. F. Smirnov, V. N. Tolstoy, Teor. Mat. Fiz. 8 (1971) 255-271 (Theor. Math. Phys. 8 (1971) 813-825); earlier R. M. Asherova, Yu. F. Smirnov, "Projection operators and Clebsch-Gordan coefficients for the group SU(3)", Nucl. Phys. B4 (1968) 399-412 (`[CIT]` via Tolstoy's review arXiv:1010.4054 `[FT]`, which lists CG theory for su(3) under refs [36]-[38]: Pluhar-Smirnov-Tolstoy preprint 1981, Czech. J. Phys. B35 (1985) 593, J. Phys. A 19 (1986) 21). The images P_mu(v (x) hw') of the slices are the highest-weight vectors, so the method is the *inverse* of the slice map (inference, not stated in this form in any paper I read). CPV Appendix A uses exactly this to build a basis of M_m (§1). These works give analytic formulas and symbolic algorithms, not a large-scale numerical engine.
* *Extremal-factor chain in the SU(3) canonical basis.* D. Braunschweig, "Reduced SU(3) coefficients of fractional parentage", Comput. Phys. Commun. 14 (1978) 109-112 (`[CIT]` via Williams, UNVERIFIED content), "Braunschweig's conjecture"; proved by H. T. Williams, arXiv:nucl-th/9309023 `[FT]` (journal UNVERIFIED): for a HW coupled state, the coefficients with one factor at its highest-weight state form a chain (j = 0..jmax, "S = Smax - j" families with two-term recursions) of length jmax+1 **at least the multiplicity**, and the outer multiplicity is resolved by setting the first rho-1 members to zero (triangular pattern). This is the SU(3) GT-basis version of "slice dimension w >= m, M_mu inside the slice space, triangular canonical resolution". Williams' own code (ANSI C) uses the recursion; Williams-Wynne, Comput. Phys. 8 (1994) 355-359 `[CIT]`.
* *Biedenharn-Louck-Hecht (BLH) canonical resolution* (Biedenharn-Giovannini-Louck, J. Math. Phys. 8 (1967) 691 `[CIT]`; Louck-Biedenharn CMP 8 (1968) 89 `[CIT]`): the vanishing conditions on reduced coupling coefficients (their eq. 30 in Herko et al.) impose an upper-triangular pattern of zeros, "imposed by row reduction followed by Gram-Schmidt" (Herko et al. footnote 2 `[FT]`). Same chain structure as above.
* *Vector coherent state (VCS) method.* D. J. Rowe, C. Bahri, J. Math. Phys. 41 (2000) 6544-6565 (DOI 10.1063/1.1286768) `[AB]` and the code paper Bahri, Rowe, Draayer, Comput. Phys. Commun. 159 (2004) 121-143 `[FT]`: "highest weight states in a tensor product space are easily identified by VCS methods"; the HW states are parametrised by a small set of parameters {a_si} whose linear system (rectangular, Gaussian elimination) has exactly rho_max solutions. D. J. Rowe, J. Repka, J. Math. Phys. 38 (1997) 4363-4388 `[AB]`: shift tensors on VCS wave functions, "simpler to determine the coefficients for highest weight states first". This is the closest in spirit to "reduce the HW problem to a small linear system", in the SU(2)-coupled formalism.
* *Draayer-Akiyama.* J. Math. Phys. 14 (1973) 1904-1912 (DOI 10.1063/1.1666267) `[AB]`: according to the 2026 description of the algorithm (Herko et al., `[FT]`, §3.1) it uses the Racah/Hecht method of infinitesimal generators for recurrences, with *seed* (extremal) coefficients obtained by a "building-up process" from couplings of lower outer multiplicity, in the SU(3) > U(1)xSU(2) basis. It does **not** form a null space and does not use the PRV conditions; it does not "effectively do the slice reduction".
* *General-N:* Alex et al. (our benchmark) solve the full null space on the weight space, §6.

**Relation to us:** same theorem; our engine uses its simplest linear-algebra form (two string conditions on the slice, exact over Q) and then reduces the Gaudin vertex operator to the slice (24 of 36 terms). I found no prior numerical implementation of this specific form. The sentence in the notes "what we add is only its use as an algorithm" is defensible but should cite Braunschweig/Williams, Rowe-Bahri and the extremal-projector school as prior algorithmic reductions of the same type.

## 3. C2: the operator

### 3a. Is V the Moshinsky-Nagel operator Omega? **No; V = X of PST/CPV (yes).**

* **Nagel-Moshinsky, J. Math. Phys. 6 (1965) 682-694, DOI 10.1063/1.1704326 `[AB]`**: "Operators that lower or raise the irreducible vector spaces of U_{n-1} contained in an irreducible vector space of U_n": polynomial functions of the U_n generators that change the U_{n-1} label (step operators, in the family later developed by Zhelobenko and Mickelsson). Nothing to do with multiplicity-resolving invariants of U(g) (x) U(g). **The brief's premise is not supported.**
* **Bargmann-Moshinsky Omega.** The cubic operator of V. Bargmann, M. Moshinsky, Nucl. Phys. 23 (1961) 177 ("Group theory of harmonic oscillators II. The integrals of motion for the quadrupole-quadrupole interaction") is the SU(3) > SO(3) missing-label operator (Elliott chain), `[CIT]` via Crampe-Shaaban Kabakibo-Vinet, arXiv:2112.09736 `[FT]` (DOI 10.1142/S0217751X22500385): "The operator x was first introduced in [Bargmann-Moshinsky], while y can be traced back to lecture notes by Racah [1964]". Judd-Miller-Patera-Winternitz, J. Math. Phys. 15 (1974) 1787-1799 `[AB]`: exactly two independent O(3) scalars (degree 3 and 4) in U(su3) beyond the Casimirs. **Different problem** (reduction SU(3) > SO(3) of one irrep) but the *same* algebra: CPV21 and CSKV observe that the centraliser of so(3) in U(su(3)) and the diagonal centraliser Z_2(sl3) satisfy the same Lehrer-Racah relations, and CSKV Remark 2: "the matrix elements of x and y [in the Bargmann-Moshinsky basis, tridiagonal] have the same form as those of the missing label operators in the two fold tensor product of su(3)".
* **Moshinsky, Rev. Mod. Phys. 34 (1962) 813, DOI 10.1103/RevModPhys.34.813 `[AB]`** (OSTI abstract: "A class of Wigner coefficients for SU3 ... Kronecker product of two irreducible representations"; no missing label in the abstract); and J. Math. Phys. 4 (1963) 1128 (title UNVERIFIED): treated through boson/complementary-group (U(4) > U(2)xU(2)) realisations. Pan-Draayer (J. Math. Phys. 39 (1998) 5631, arXiv:quant-ph/9704014 `[FT]`): "to resolve the problem for the SU(3), **Hecht [Nucl. Phys. 62 (1965) 1] proposed an external labeling operator of third order, an operator that may be related to the one proposed by Moshinsky** in terms of the complementary U(4) > U(2)xU(2) chain; Alisauskas and Kulish also proposed a fourth-order external labeling operator, a form suggested by Sharp in a study of Yang-Baxter equations." I could not access Hecht's paper; **that Hecht's cubic operator equals X is plausible but UNVERIFIED.** Whether Moshinsky's own operator Omega/Phi belong to the tensor-product problem is also unverified; I found the pair Omega (3rd) / Phi (4th) only in the SU(3) > SO(3) literature.
* **The operator X.** PST86 (labelling operator from the generators; AB) and CPV (read). *Verified by me* (Appendix A): V and X_m have the same spectrum up to the constant kappa_mu - eta/2 in four sectors. So (a): **V = X + (Casimir-valued constant), yes.** The constant is exactly the project's "mean of V over the copies" (the trace of X_m vanishes in the three cases checked).
* Other resolutions in the literature (for completeness): Mathur, Rathor, Sreeraj, arXiv:1906.10410 `[FT]` (Schwinger-boson SU(3)xSU(3) invariants forming so(4,2), a family of operators "any one of which" separates the copies; their remark that the remaining cubic invariants are not needed); Pan-Draayer (complementary group U(2n-2), closed multiplicity formulas for SU(3), SU(4)); Alisauskas' pseudo-canonical schemes (`[CIT]`).

### 3b. Tridiagonal / Jacobi form with closed entries: **PARTIALLY KNOWN** (confidence 80%)

* PST86 `[AB]`+`[CIT]`: matrix of X on M_m "quasi-tridiagonal", eigenvalues distinct; basis built with the extremal projector.
* CPV23 `[FT]`: tridiagonal in the omega-basis, closed forms, X and Y simultaneously tridiagonal; BM-basis analogue for SU(3) > SO(3) in CSKV.
* **Ours:** tridiagonal on the **GT basis of the weight space V_Lambda[mu-Lambda']** (chain in m1, eq. 8, ten linear factors over (x+1)(x+2)^2(x+3), positive products, genuine Jacobi matrix on the E12-cut sub-chain), with M_mu a sub-chain (E12) or a spectral subspace/reflected sub-chain (E23). **Not found** in the literature I could access. Different basis (different cyclic vector) from CPV's: the products differ (CPV's are sextic with negative sign; for (21,14,7) CPV's products are j^3 (j-8)^3, ours ten-factor) so the two Jacobi forms are related by a nondiagonal change of basis (I did not compute it). The simplicity-of-spectrum argument is cleaner in our basis (positive products), a small point worth making.

### 3c. slice_k = p_k(V): **NOT FOUND in this form** (confidence 60%; PST86 not read)

CPV give X omega_m = alpha_m omega_{m-1} + beta_m omega_m + delta_m omega_{m+1}, so the eigenvectors of X in the omega-basis are the three-term-recurrence polynomials in the eigenvalue; but omega_m are extremal-projector images, and CPV never expand them in the product (GT) basis, so no statement about CG coefficients. PST86 says an "algebraic algorithm" is presented; if it gives the CG coefficients of an X-eigencopy as polynomials in the eigenvalue it would pre-empt (c); this must be checked in the paper. The su(2) prototype (CG coefficients = Hahn polynomials; CPV Sect. 1.5, Granovskii-Zhedanov; J. Koornwinder, Nieuw Arch. Wisk. 29 (1981) 140-155 `[MEM]`, UNVERIFIED) shows the structure is the expected one; the new content is that for gl(3) it holds on the **slice itself in the GT basis**, with closed-form coefficients valid for any Lambda, plus the cut structure.

## 4. C3: Gaudin / shift-of-argument eigenbasis as the canonical multiplicity basis

**Verdict: KNOWN at the structural level; the sl(3) two-site case with the labelling operator is explicit in CS-Musso 2013 and CPV; the GT-basis explicit form is not.** Confidence 85%.

* **Vinberg's problem.** E. B. Vinberg, "On certain commutative subalgebras of a universal enveloping algebra", Izv. Akad. Nauk SSSR Ser. Mat. 54:1 (1990) 3-25; Math. USSR-Izv. 36:1 (1991) 1-22 (`[AB]` via catalogue records; also cited as "Some commutative subalgebras..."): lift the Mishchenko-Fomenko subalgebras of S(g) to commutative subalgebras of U(g).
* **Rybnikov, "Argument shift method and the Gaudin model", Funct. Anal. Appl. 40 (2006) 188-199 (Russian 30-43), DOI 10.1007/s10688-006-0030-3, arXiv:math/0606380 `[AB]`**: for g = sl_r the commutative subalgebras "have simple spectrum in tensor products of finite-dimensional g-modules for generic mu and z_i".
* **Feigin, Frenkel, Rybnikov, Duke Math. J. 155 (2010) 337-363, DOI 10.1215/00127094-2010-057, arXiv:0712.1183 `[AB]`**: generic diagonalisability on finite-dimensional modules, spectra = monodromy-free opers (irregular singularity), and a multi-point generalisation.
* **Rybnikov, "A proof of the Gaudin Bethe Ansatz conjecture", arXiv:1608.04625 `[AB]`** (IMRN 2020, UNVERIFIED): the Gaudin algebra has a cyclic vector in the space of singular vectors of any tensor product of finite-dimensional irreps, for all z, hence simple spectrum and joint eigenvalues in bijection with monodromy-free opers with regular singularities at z_1..z_N, infinity. This covers our two-site multiplicity spaces (any distinct z_1, z_2; for two sites the parameter is irrelevant, so the eigenbasis is canonical up to scale).
* **Mukhin, Tarasov, Varchenko, J. Amer. Math. Soc. 22 (2009) 909-940, arXiv:0711.4079 `[AB]`**: the Bethe algebra on a multiplicity space is the coregular representation of the scheme-theoretic intersection of Schubert varieties, simplicity of spectrum for real parameters. Related: MTV, Glasgow Math. J. 51 (2009) 137-150 (separation of variables and completeness for gl_N Gaudin, `[CIT]`), arXiv:0910.4690 (Bethe vectors for different critical points are linearly independent, `[AB]`). Mukhin-Schechtman-Tarasov-Varchenko, arXiv:math/0609428 `[AB]` (new form of Bethe equations and a separation of variables for the sl_3 Gaudin model).
* **Halacheva, Kamnitzer, Rybnikov, Weekes, Duke Math. J. 169 (2020) 2337-2419, arXiv:1708.05105 `[AB]`**: for real parameter the Gaudin algebra acts with simple spectrum on the multiplicity space and gives an eigenbasis; its monodromy gives the cactus group action (Etingof's conjecture, crystals). For two sites there is no parameter and no monodromy; the statement is vacuous for our case but is the correct general framework for "Gaudin eigenbasis = canonical".
* **sl(3) two sites with the missing label, explicitly:** Campoamor-Stursberg, Musso, J. Phys. A 46 (2013) 335201, DOI 10.1088/1751-8113/46/33/335201 `[AB]` (OSTI record): "the spectral invariants of the Lax matrix correspond to linear combinations of the labelling operators" of sl(3) in sl(3) (+) sl(3), applied to the degeneracy separation of the CG series. CPV23 §1.6 `[FT]` (Bethe ansatz). Jarvis-Zhang, J. Phys. A 38 (2005) L219 (the O(3)-invariant Bethe subalgebra of the twisted Yangian resolves GL(3) > O(3), `[CIT]` via CSKV) is the SU(3) > SO(3) analogue.
* Not found: any paper writing the Gaudin eigenbasis for SU(3) in the GT basis, or computing CG coefficients from it.

**Relation:** our identification "SoV copies = eigenbasis of V = eigenbasis of the cubic two-site Gaudin Hamiltonian" is a restatement of CS-Musso + PST/CPV. The Gaudin side adds the Q-system language, which is what we exploit.

## 5. C4: accessory parameter, oper dictionary, Heun/Heine-Stieltjes

**Verdict: PARTIALLY KNOWN.** Confidence 75%.

* **Dictionary eigenvalues <-> oper coefficients.** B. Feigin, E. Frenkel, N. Reshetikhin, Commun. Math. Phys. 166 (1994) 27-62, hep-th/9402022 `[AB]`; E. Frenkel, "Opers on the projective line, flag manifolds and Bethe Ansatz", Mosc. Math. J. 4 (2004) 655-705, arXiv:math/0308269 `[AB]`: "the eigenvalues of the Gaudin hamiltonians are encoded by opers ... regular singularities at the marked points with prescribed residues and trivial monodromy"; E. Frenkel, "Gaudin model and opers", Progr. Math. 237 (2005) 1-58, arXiv:math/0407524 `[AB]`. The statement "the cubic Gaudin eigenvalue is the accessory parameter of the gl(3) Fuchsian oper" is therefore the standard dictionary, not new. (Standard Fuchsian counting, INFERRED: a third-order Fuchsian operator with three singular points has exactly (n-1)(n-2)/2 = 1 accessory parameter, matching one missing-label eigenvalue per copy.)
* **Existence/completeness.** Rybnikov 2020 (above): number of monodromy-free opers = dimension of the multiplicity space; this is why the number of Q-system roots equals the multiplicity (our 113/113 check). * **sl(2) analogue (correction of the premise).** The two-site sl(2) Gaudin model is multiplicity-free and its oper has only three regular singular points (hypergeometric, no accessory parameter); the Heun equation (four singular points) corresponds to **three** sites. ## 6. C5: closed-form multiplicity

**Verdict: KNOWN; equivalent to the classical closed formulas.** Confidence 90% that it is equivalent (each source is a proven formula, ours agrees with Racah-Speiser on 39 608 weights); I did not verify term-by-term identities.

* **Our rule:** m = w1 = min(l1, s-l3, mu1-l2) - max(l2, s-l2, t, s-t) + 1 if mu2 >= l1+l3, else m = w2 (reflected chain).
* **Coleman 1964** (J. Math. Phys. 5 (1964) 1343, `[CIT]` via Mathur et al., UNVERIFIED content), **Speiser, Mandel'tsveig, Resnikoff 1967** (J. Math. Phys. 8 (1967) 63, `[CIT]`): classical SU(3) multiplicity formulas.
* **M. F. O'Reilly, J. Math. Phys. 23 (1982) 2022-2028, DOI 10.1063/1.525258 `[AB]`**: closed formula in p,q,r,s for the decomposition and the multiplicity of [m,n] in [p,q] x [r,s] (full text not read).
* **Begin, Mathieu, Walton, Mod. Phys. Lett. A 7 (1992) 3255-3265 `[CIT]`**, stated in full in Barker-Swinarski-Vogelstein-Wu, arXiv:1408.4353 `[FT]` (Theorem 2.1): N = k0max - k0min + 1 with k0max = min{A,B}, k0min = max{a+b, c+d, e+f, A-min(a,c,f), B-min(b,d,e)}, A,B the two triality combinations. In the level-infinity limit this is the tensor product multiplicity: **"min of two minus max of nine, plus one"**, with the regime split A <= B / A >= B.
* **CPV23 eq. (10)** `[FT]` (attributed there to PST86 or O'Reilly): d = min of 18 numbers arranged as two 3x3 magic squares; "if n <= l the multiplicity is the minimum of the left 9 elements, while if l <= n it is the minimum of the right 9": **two regimes, never mixed**, as in ours. With the CPV conventions the regime boundary is l - n = l2 + l2' - mu2 (my derivation from their definitions), i.e. mu2 vs 2 l2 for two copies of Lambda; our boundary mu2 vs l1 + l3 coincides on the benchmark family [2a,a,0] (l1 + l3 = 2 l2) and differs in general (both are valid where the two chains give equal lengths).
* Also: Pan-Draayer (J. Math. Phys. 39 (1998) 5631) "new explicit formulae for the multiplicity for SU(3) and SU(4)" `[FT]` (abstract); E. Rassart, J. Combin. Theory A 107 (2004) 161-179 (polynomial LR coefficients for GL3, `[CIT]` via Briand-Rosas, bibliographic data from memory); E. Briand, M. Rosas, arXiv:2004.04995 `[FT]` (144 symmetries); hive/BZ-triangle counts (Berenstein-Zelevinsky).

**Relation:** same formula in different clothes. The only possibly new element is the *interpretation* (the two string conditions as two cuts of one Jacobi chain, never both active, the E23 cut being the Weyl-reflected E12 cut). Do not present the formula as new.

**What the numbers imply for [14,7,0] x [14,7,0].** In Dynkin labels this is (7,7) x (7,7); its 113 couplings have Sigma_w = 28 + (lambda3+mu3) between 28 and 56. If the Fig. 8 reading is right, ndsu3lib returns *all* SU(3) > U(1)xSU(2) reduced coefficients of a coupling (every state of the coupled irrep, every outer multiplicity) in about 1-5 ms, so **all 113 couplings in roughly 0.3-1 s on a 2014 Xeon core** (my extrapolation; nobody has published this run). Our slice engine: 0.10 s (slices), 0.17 s (HW vectors written), on an i7-12700KF. The honest comparison is therefore "same order of magnitude", for different objects:
  * theirs: all SU(2)-reduced coefficients for all states, BLH canonical multiplicity basis; ours: HW vectors only, SoV (V-eigen) basis. Converting their output to a GT-basis HW vector should cost one pass over n_W entries (SU(2) CG coefficients times reduced coefficients), the same output-size bound we have (INFERENCE).
  * they never solve a null space of size n_W either: their cost is set by the number of SU(2)-reduced coefficients of the coupling, which is smaller than n_W by roughly the typical SU(2) multiplet size (INFERENCE, not measured).
  * range: AD fails beyond Sigma_w = 65; ndsu3lib is tested to 81; [28,14,0]^2 has Sigma_w up to 112, outside every verified range (but multiprecision options exist, unmeasured).

**Verdict for the paper:** quote "x60 against the repaired general-N null-space code, x1200 against the distributed one" and say that the SU(3)-specific D-A lineage (ndsu3lib/SU3lib) was not benchmarked; better, **run ndsu3lib on the 113 couplings of (7,7)x(7,7) on the same machine** and report both. Also note that, in the language of the slice, the BLH canonical copies are triangular with respect to the same chain (Williams/Braunschweig zeros pattern); since V_red is tridiagonal, the BLH flag is the Krylov flag of V_red from one end of the chain, so BLH basis and SoV basis should be related by the Lanczos/orthogonal-polynomial transformation (INFERENCE, not tested). That would be a useful, citable bridge to the community that uses the D-A codes.

## 9. Premises of the brief that did not check out

1. Nagel-Moshinsky J. Math. Phys. 6 (1965) 682 is about step operators of U_{n-1} in U_n, not a missing-label operator.
2. Partensky-Maguin J. Math. Phys. 19 (1978) 511-525 is "The SU(4) > SU(2) x SU(2) chain" (DOI 10.1063/1.523687), the missing label of the supermultiplet problem, not "The missing label problem for SU(3) x SU(3) > SU(3)". 3. "Two-site Gaudin = Heun equation" holds for sl(2) with three sites; two sites give a hypergeometric oper without accessory parameter. The sl(3) two-site oper (three singular points, third order) is the first case with an accessory parameter.
4. "Omega (cubic) and Phi (quartic)" as Moshinsky's tensor-product operators: I found Omega/Phi (x, y) only for SU(3) > SO(3) (Bargmann-Moshinsky, Racah, Judd et al.); for tensor products the cubic and quartic operators are X and Y of PST/CPV.
