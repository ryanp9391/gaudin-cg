# gl(3), L=1, adjoint: SoV in the new conventions (clean)

This is the clean version of `Companion-B-C-diagonalize.wb`. It keeps only the final formulas and the checks that establish them; the exploration (failed operator forms, ansatz scans) stays in the original. It is self-contained: the first cell clears the kernel. Full write-up: `Companion-B-C-diagonalize-clean.md` / `.tex`.

**Conventions.**
- **Lax.** $\mathcal L_{ij}(u)=u\,\delta_{ij}+h\,E_{ij}$, with $T_{ij}(u)=\mathcal L_{ij}(u-\theta)$ and one site.
- **Twist.** Companion form $G(z)=\begin{pmatrix}\chi_1&-\chi_2&\chi_3\\1&0&0\\0&1&0\end{pmatrix}$, with $\chi_k$ the elementary symmetric polynomials of $z$, acting on the right: $T^G=\mathcal L\,G$.
- **Quantum minors.** Rows are antisymmetrized and column $k$ is evaluated at $u-(k-1)h$. The transfer matrices are $t_a=\sum_{|I|=a}\mathrm{qmin}_{I,I}[T^G]$.
- **Factors.** $\nu_n(u)=u-\theta+h\lambda_n$, with rep $\lambda=(2,1,0)$.
- **Q-functions.** *Direct* $Q_i=z_i^{-u/h}q_i$ and *dual* $Q^j=z_j^{+u/h}q^j$, with the $q$'s monic. The shift notation is $Q^{[2k]}(u)=Q(u+kh)$.

**Numeric point** (one point; every shift is in units of $h$): $h=1/2$, $\theta=2/7$, $z=(3/2,5/4,2/3)$ so $\chi_3=5/4$, and second twist $w=(5/2,7/3,3/5)$.

The last cell tabulates every check.

## 2. B and C operators and the SoV bases
$B(u)=T_{11}(u)\,\mathrm{qmin}_{12,12}[T](u+h)+T_{21}(u)\,\mathrm{qmin}_{12,13}[T](u+h)$, and $C(u)$ is the same with the minors at $u$; both use the **untwisted** $T$.

The bases are labelled by GT patterns $\mu=\{\{\mu_{11}\},\{\mu_{21},\mu_{22}\}\}$:
- **$\langle x_\mu|$.** Built from $\langle x_0|=e_1^T$ (pattern $\{\{2\},\{2,1\}\}$) by fused $t$'s at $\theta-2h$. They are B-eigencovectors with eigenvalue $(u-\theta+h\mu_{11}+h)(u-\theta+h\mu_{21})(u-\theta+h\mu_{22})$.
- **$|y_\mu\rangle$.** Built from $|y_{\rm low}\rangle=e_8$ (pattern $\{\{1\},\{0,0\}\}$) by shifted $t$'s at $\theta$. They are C-eigenvectors with eigenvalue $(u-\theta+h\mu_{11})(u-\theta+h\mu_{21})(u-\theta+h\mu_{22}-h)$.

The overlap matrix $\langle x_\mu|y_\nu\rangle$ is triangular and independent of $z$, with $\langle x_0|y_0\rangle=9h^4$, where $y_0=y_{\{2\},\{2,1\}}$.

## 3. Frames: companion and diagonal twist
$\Omega_L=e_1^TP_{pp}$ is the companion vacuum, with eigenvalue $\tau_{\rm vac}(u)=\sum_jz_j\nu_j(u)$. The Gauss factors $P_{\rm aux}$ (auxiliary space) and $P_{\rm phys}$ (physical space) satisfy:
- $P_{\rm aux}G(z)P_{\rm aux}^{-1}=\mathrm{diag}(z)$;
- $P_{\rm phys}\,t_1(z)\,P_{\rm phys}^{-1}=\sum_iz_i\mathcal L_{ii}(u-\theta)$, which is justified by gl(3) invariance of the Lax operator.

The second twist $w$ written in the frame where $G(z)$ is diagonal is $G_{P,zw}=P_{\rm aux}G(w)P_{\rm aux}^{-1}$. Its transfer matrix `tGPzw` has the vacuum covector $\Omega_{zw}$.

## 4. Eigenstates, Baxter equations, QQ relation
**Direct equation** (on $Q_i=z_i^{-x/h}q_i$, with $\deg q_i=\lambda_1-\mu_i$):
$$\nu_1(x)\nu_1(x+h)\nu_1(x+2h)Q(x-h)-t_1(x)\nu_1(x+h)\nu_1(x+2h)Q(x)+t_2(x+h)\nu_1(x+2h)Q(x+h)-t_3(x+2h)Q(x+2h)=0.$$
**Dual equation** (on $Q^j=z_j^{+x/h}q^j$, with $\deg q^j=\mu_j-\lambda_3$), in uniform form:
$$\nu_3(x)\nu_3(x-h)\nu_3(x-2h)Q(x+h)-t_1(x)\nu_3(x-h)\nu_3(x-2h)Q(x)+t_2(x)\nu_3(x-2h)Q(x-h)-t_3(x)Q(x-2h)=0.$$
**QQ relation** (polynomial parts; the exponentials differ by $\chi_3^{-u/h}$):
$$Q_a(u)Q_b(u+h)-Q_b(u)Q_a(u+h)\propto\Big(\tfrac1{z_b}-\tfrac1{z_a}\Big)F_\lambda(u)Q^c(u),\qquad F_\lambda=\prod_{n=\lambda_2+1}^{\lambda_1}(u-\theta+nh).$$
The weight $\mu$ of a state is read off from $t_1(\theta)=h\sum_iz_i\mu_i$, i.e. in the frame where the twist is diagonal.

## 5. Wronskian (quantum Weyl) formula for every $T_\lambda$
Let $D_{k_1k_2k_3}(x)=\det_{j,c}Q^j(x+k_ch)$, with $z_j^{x/h}$ stripped from each row. Expanding the vanishing $4\times4$ determinant (rows $Q^1,Q^2,Q^3,Q$; column shifts $+h,0,-h,-2h$) along its last row reproduces the dual equation, so
$$t_1=\nu_3(x)\frac{D_{1,-1,-2}}{D_{0,-1,-2}},\quad t_2=\nu_3(x)\nu_3(x-h)\frac{D_{1,0,-2}}{D_{0,-1,-2}},\quad t_3=\nu_3(x)\nu_3(x-h)\nu_3(x-2h)\frac{D_{1,0,-1}}{D_{0,-1,-2}}.$$
More generally, with CBR $T_\lambda(u)=\det_{1\le i,j\le\lambda_1}t_{\lambda'_j+i-j}(u+(i-1)h)$ ($t_0=1$; $t_a=0$ for $a<0$ or $a>3$):
$$T_\lambda(x)=\prod_{(r,c)\in\lambda}\nu_3\big(x+(c-r)h\big)\;\frac{D_{\lambda_1,\lambda_2-1,\lambda_3-2}(x)}{D_{0,-1,-2}(x)}.$$

## 6. Reduction at $x=\theta-2h$
Write $v(p)=(Q^1,Q^2,Q^3)(p)$ for the dual columns.
- **Degeneracies.** The dual equation at $x=\theta+h$ gives $v(\theta)\parallel v(\theta-h)$. At $x=\theta-h$ it then makes $v(\theta-3h),v(\theta-2h),v(\theta-h)$ dependent. So $v(\theta-3h),\dots,v(\theta)$ lie in one 2-plane.
- **Reduction.** At $x=\theta-2h$ every $3\times3$ ratio collapses:
$$T_\lambda(\theta-2h)=\begin{cases}P_\lambda(\theta-2h)\,\dfrac{\det_2[v(\theta+(\lambda_1-2)h),\,v(\theta+(\lambda_2-3)h)]}{\det_2[v(\theta-2h),\,v(\theta-3h)]}&\lambda_3=0,\ \lambda_1\le2,\\[4pt]0&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}$$
  for any choice of the two rows. Only $(1),(1,1),(2),(2,1)$ survive; $(2,2)$ vanishes through the parallel pair.

## 7. SoV wavefunctions
- **Kets.** $|\Psi\rangle$ is a right eigenvector, normalized by $\langle x_0|\Psi\rangle=1$. Since every $\langle x_\mu|$ is $\langle x_0|$ times $t$'s at $\theta-2h$, $\langle x_\mu|\Psi\rangle$ is a product of $T_\lambda(\theta-2h)$; for example $\langle x_{\{2\},\{2,0\}}|\Psi\rangle=T_{20}/T_{10}$. With Section 6 this gives
$$\langle x_\mu|\Psi\rangle=N_\mu\,\frac{\det_2[v(x_{21}),v(x_1)]\,v_k(x_{22})}{\det_2[v(\theta-2h),v(\theta-3h)]\,v_k(\theta-h)},$$
  where $x_1=\theta-(\mu_{11}+1)h$, $x_{21}=\theta-\mu_{21}h$ and $x_{22}=\theta-\mu_{22}h$ are the B-roots.
- **Bras.** $\langle\Psi|$ is a right eigenvector of the transposed $t$'s, normalized by $\langle\Psi|y_0\rangle=1$. With the direct columns $u(p)=(Q_1,Q_2,Q_3)(p)$, which satisfy $u(\theta-2h)\parallel u(\theta-h)$,
$$\frac{\langle\Psi|y_\mu\rangle}{\langle\Psi|y_{\rm low}\rangle}=N'_\mu\,\frac{\det_2[u(y_{21}),u(y_{22})]\,u_k(y_1)}{(\text{same at }y_{\rm low})},$$
  where $y_1=\theta-\mu_{11}h$, $y_{21}=\theta-\mu_{21}h$ and $y_{22}=\theta-(\mu_{22}-1)h$ are the C-roots.

$N_\mu$ and $N'_\mu$ do not depend on the state, and the choice of rows and $k$ is free.

## 8. Functional orthogonality and norms
**Bracket.** $\langle f\rangle=\sum_{n=\lambda_3}^{\lambda_1}\frac{f(\theta-nh)}{\prod_{m\ne n}(m-n)h}$, the residues of $\rho=\prod_{n=\lambda_3}^{\lambda_1}(x-\theta+nh)^{-1}$.

**Derivation.** Adjointness of the direct and dual operators under $\mu=\prod_{n=\lambda_3}^{\lambda_1+2}(x-\theta+nh)^{-1}$, with $\varphi=\frac{\nu_1(x+h)\nu_1(x+2h)}{\nu_3(x-h)\nu_3(x-2h)}$, together with $\delta t_1=$ const, $\delta t_2$ linear and $\delta t_3=0$, gives for each $(i,j)$:
$$-\delta I_1\langle P^jQ_i\rangle+a\langle P^jQ_i^{[2]}\rangle+b\langle P^{j[-2]}Q_i\rangle=0.$$
So $\det N$, the $3\times3$ determinant with rows $(i,j)\in\{(1,3),(1,2),(2,3)\}$ and these three columns, vanishes between different states with the same twist.

**Orientation.** $P^j$ are the **ket's** dual $Q$'s and $Q_i$ the **bra's** direct $Q$'s, as the SoV expansion requires.

**Norm.** Let $c_0$ be the coefficient of the $(x_0,y_0)$ term $\Psi_Q(x_0)\Phi_Q(y_0)$ when $\det N$ is expanded with the $Q$'s as symbols. Then
$$\langle\Psi_A|\Psi_B\rangle=\frac{\det N[\text{ket }B\text{ dual},\ \text{bra }A\text{ direct}]}{c_0\,\langle x_0|y_0\rangle\,\Psi_Q^B(x_0)\,\Phi_Q^A(y_0)},$$
with
- $\Psi_Q(x_0)=[Q^2(\theta-2h)Q^3(\theta-3h)-Q^3(\theta-2h)Q^2(\theta-3h)]\,Q^3(\theta-h)$,
- $\Phi_Q(y_0)=[Q_1(\theta-2h)Q_2(\theta)-Q_2(\theta-2h)Q_1(\theta)]\,Q_1(\theta-2h)$,
- $c_0=1/(4h^6)$ and $\langle x_0|y_0\rangle=9h^4$.
