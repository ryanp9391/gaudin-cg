# gl(3), L=2, adjoint⊗adjoint: overlap formula in the diagonal frame

Goal: the two-site version of row 19 of `Companion-B-C-diagonalize-clean.md`, i.e. the overlap of the rotated $w$-vacuum with a diagonal-twist eigenstate written as a determinant of $Q$'s. Spec: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-01-L2-diagonal-frame-overlap-design.md`. The first code cell clears the kernel.

**Conventions** (new, as in the clean L=1 notebook).
- **Lax.** $\mathcal L_{ij}(u)=u\,\delta_{ij}+h\,E_{ij}$; two sites, $T(u)=\mathcal L^{(1)}(u-\theta_1)\,\mathcal L^{(2)}(u-\theta_2)$ (product in auxiliary space).
- **Twist.** Diagonal, $T^z=T\,\mathrm{diag}(z)$. No companion frame anywhere.
- **Quantum minors.** Rows antisymmetrized, column $k$ at $u-(k-1)h$; $t_a=\sum_{|I|=a}\mathrm{qmin}_{I,I}$.
- **Factors.** $\nu_n(u)=\prod_s(u-\theta_s+h\lambda_n)$ (the L=1 factor, multiplied over sites).
- **Q-functions.** Direct $Q_i=z_i^{-u/h}q_i$, dual $Q^j=z_j^{+u/h}q^j$, monic $q$'s.

**Numeric point** (exact rationals; 60 digits where floats enter): $h=1/2$, $\theta=(2/7,-3/5)$, $z=(3/2,5/4,2/3)$, $w=(5/2,7/3,3/5)$.

> **EH variant (2026-10-01).** This copy of `L2-diagonal-frame-overlap.wb` uses Hermitian generators $EH_{ij}=R\,E_{ij}R^{-1}$, with $EH_{ij}^T=EH_{ji}$ and $R=\sqrt M=\mathrm{diag}(1,1,1,\sqrt2,\sqrt6,3,2,3)$ in `ps` order. $M^{-1}E_{ij}^TM=E_{ji}$ with $M_1=1$ (cell 4). The changes from the E-basis notebook are:
> - **Zero tests.** Surds don't cancel canonically in exact arithmetic, so exact zero tests use `zeroQ` (`PossibleZeroQ`).
> - **$\langle x_0|y_0\rangle$.** The hard-coded $9h^4$ is replaced by the computed value, now $9h^4R_1/R_8=3h^4$ per site.
> - **New Section 1b.** It checks the Hermitian structure: $t_a^T$ equals the transfer matrix with the sites reversed.
>
> All other results are unchanged, including the value of $R$ and the 50-digit overlap formula.

J0[k_][pat_] := J0[k][pat] = Sum[pat[[k, i]], {i, 1, k}] - Sum[pat[[k - 1, i]], {i, 1, k - 1}];
Jp[k_][pat_] := Jp[k][pat] = -Sum[(Product[pat[[k, i]] - i - pat[[k + 1, j]] + j, {j, Range[k + 1]}]/Product[pat[[k, i]] - i - pat[[k, j]] + j, {j, Complement[Range[k], {i}]}])*v[ReplacePart[pat, {k, i} -> pat[[k, i]] + 1]], {i, 1, k}];
Jm[k_][pat_] := Jm[k][pat] = Sum[(Product[pat[[k, i]] - i - pat[[k - 1, j]] + j, {j, Range[k - 1]}]/Product[pat[[k, i]] - i - pat[[k, j]] + j, {j, Complement[Range[k], {i}]}])*v[ReplacePart[pat, {k, i} -> pat[[k, i]] - 1]], {i, 1, k}];
JJ[k_, l_, rep_] := JJ[k, l, rep] = Which[
  k == l, DiagonalMatrix[J0[k] /@ ps[{rep}]],
  l == k + 1, Transpose[Table[(Coefficient[Jp[k][ps[{rep}][[i]]], v[#1]] &) /@ ps[{rep}], {i, 1, Length[ps[{rep}]]}]],
  l == k - 1, Transpose[Table[(Coefficient[Jm[l][ps[{rep}][[i]]], v[#1]] &) /@ ps[{rep}], {i, 1, Length[ps[{rep}]]}]],
  l > k + 1, JJ[k, k + 1, rep] . JJ[k + 1, l, rep] - JJ[k + 1, l, rep] . JJ[k, k + 1, rep],
  l < k - 1, JJ[k, k - 1, rep] . JJ[k - 1, l, rep] - JJ[k - 1, l, rep] . JJ[k, k - 1, rep]];

## 1. Two-site transfer matrices (diagonal twist)
$t_a(u)$ has degree $\le 2a\le 6$ in $u$, so it is rebuilt exactly (Lagrange) from 7 rational sample points; each sample is an exact $64\times64$ product. Checks: the quantum determinant $t_3=\chi_3\,\nu_1(u)\nu_2(u-h)\nu_3(u-2h)$, commutativity, and weight conservation (the diagonal twist commutes with the Cartan).

### 1b. Hermitian structure with EH
Since $EH_{ij}^T=EH_{ji}$, transposing the physical space turns $\mathcal L^{(s)}_{ij}$ into $\mathcal L^{(s)}_{ji}$, and it reverses the order of the operator products. At L=1 this makes $t_a(\mathrm{diag}\,z)$ symmetric. At L=2 the site order in the monodromy is reversed instead: $t_a(u)^T$ is the transfer matrix of $T^{\rm rev}(u)=\mathcal L^{(2)}(u-\theta_2)\,\mathcal L^{(1)}(u-\theta_1)$, which is not $t_a$ itself. Both statements are checked at a sample $u$.

## 3. Baxter equations and QQ relation at L=2
The L=1 equations with each $\nu_n$ replaced by the two-site product $\nu_n(x)=\prod_s(x-\theta_s+h\lambda_n)$:
$$\nu_1(x)\nu_1(x+h)\nu_1(x+2h)Q(x-h)-t_1(x)\nu_1(x+h)\nu_1(x+2h)Q(x)+t_2(x+h)\nu_1(x+2h)Q(x+h)-t_3(x+2h)Q(x+2h)=0,$$
$$\nu_3(x)\nu_3(x-h)\nu_3(x-2h)Q(x+h)-t_1(x)\nu_3(x-h)\nu_3(x-2h)Q(x)+t_2(x)\nu_3(x-2h)Q(x-h)-t_3(x)Q(x-2h)=0.$$
Expected degrees: $\deg q_i=L\lambda_1-\mu_i$, $\deg q^j=\mu_j-L\lambda_3$. QQ factor $F_\lambda=\prod_s\prod_{n=\lambda_2+1}^{\lambda_1}(u-\theta_s+nh)$. The solver searches the minimal degree up to 6, so the degree rule is a test, not an input.

===== cell 12 (kind 2) =====
opDir[tt_, Q_, x_] := nuP[1][x] nuP[1][x + hN] nuP[1][x + 2 hN] Q[x - hN] - (tt[[1]] /. u -> x) nuP[1][x + hN] nuP[1][x + 2 hN] Q[x] + (tt[[2]] /. u -> x + hN) nuP[1][x + 2 hN] Q[x + hN] - (tt[[3]] /. u -> x + 2 hN) Q[x + 2 hN];
opDual[tt_, Q_, x_] := nuP[3][x] nuP[3][x - hN] nuP[3][x - 2 hN] Q[x + hN] - (tt[[1]] /. u -> x) nuP[3][x - hN] nuP[3][x - 2 hN] Q[x] + (tt[[2]] /. u -> x) nuP[3][x - 2 hN] Q[x - hN] - (tt[[3]] /. u -> x) Q[x - 2 hN];
(* minimal-degree monic q with Q = zz^(s x/h) q solving op; {} if none up to degree 6 *)
solveQ[op_, tt_, zz_, s_] := Module[{c, eqs, ns, res = {}},
  Do[If[res === {},
    eqs = CoefficientList[Chop[Expand[op[tt, Function[yy, zz^(s (yy - X)/hN) Sum[c[j] yy^j, {j, 0, d}]], X]], 10^-40], X];
    ns = If[eqs === {}, {UnitVector[d + 1, d + 1]}, NullSpace[N[Table[Coefficient[e, c[j]], {e, eqs}, {j, 0, d}], prec], Tolerance -> 10^-30]];
    If[Length[ns] > 0, res = Expand[#/Coefficient[#, X, d]] &[Sum[ns[[1, j + 1]] X^j, {j, 0, d}]]]], {d, 0, 6}]; res];
{qTime, {qd, qu}} = AbsoluteTiming[{Table[solveQ[opDir, vals[[A]], zN[[i]], -1], {A, nS}, {i, 3}], Table[solveQ[opDual, vals[[A]], zN[[j]], 1], {A, nS}, {j, 3}]}];
checks["all direct and dual Q's found"] = FreeQ[{qd, qu}, {}];
checks["deg q_i = Lλ1 - μ_i"] = And @@ Flatten[Table[Exponent[qd[[A, i]], X] == nL Λ[[1]] - muS[[A, i]], {A, nS}, {i, 3}]];
checks["deg q^j = μ_j - Lλ3"] = And @@ Flatten[Table[Exponent[qu[[A, j]], X] == muS[[A, j]] - nL Λ[[3]], {A, nS}, {j, 3}]];
Flam[x_] := Product[x - thN[[s]] + n hN, {s, nL}, {n, Λ[[2]] + 1, Λ[[1]]}];
checks["QQ: Q_a Q_b^[2] - Q_b Q_a^[2] = (1/z_b-1/z_a) F Q^c"] = Max[Table[With[{q = qd[[A]], qc = qu[[A, abc[[3]]]], a = abc[[1]], b = abc[[2]]},
     maxc[q[[a]] (q[[b]] /. X -> X + hN)/zN[[b]] - q[[b]] (q[[a]] /. X -> X + hN)/zN[[a]] - (1/zN[[b]] - 1/zN[[a]]) Flam[X] qc, X]], {A, nS}, {abc, {{1, 2, 3}, {2, 3, 1}, {3, 1, 2}}}]];
Grid[Prepend[Append[Table[{k, checks[k]}, {k, {"all direct and dual Q's found", "deg q_i = Lλ1 - μ_i", "deg q^j = μ_j - Lλ3", "QQ: Q_a Q_b^[2] - Q_b Q_a^[2] = (1/z_b-1/z_a) F Q^c"}}], {"solve time (s)", qTime}], {"check", "value"}], Frame -> All, Alignment -> Left]

## 4. The rotated $w$-vacuum bra
From the L=1 notebook: $P_{\rm aux}G(z)P_{\rm aux}^{-1}=\mathrm{diag}(z)$, and the second twist written in this diagonal frame is $G'=P_{\rm aux}G(w)P_{\rm aux}^{-1}$. At L=1 its vacuum covector is $\Omega_{zw}=e_1^T e^{r_1E_{12}}e^{r_2E_{13}}e^{r_3E_{23}}$. A global rotation acts as $g\otimes g$, so at L=2 the claim is $\Omega^{(2)}=\Omega_{zw}\otimes\Omega_{zw}$, a left eigenvector of every $t_a(G')$ with $t_1=\sum_jw_j\nu_j(u)$. Its direct $Q$'s, $Q_{i,{\rm vac}}=w_i^{-u/h}q_i$, come from the direct Baxter equation with these eigenvalues.

===== cell 15 (kind 1) =====
## 5. Two-site bracket and functional orthogonality
**Bracket.** $\langle f\rangle=\sum_{s,n}\mathrm{Res}_{x=\theta_s-nh}\,\rho f$ with $\rho=\prod_s\prod_{n=\lambda_3}^{\lambda_1}(x-\theta_s+nh)^{-1}$; at L=1 this is the clean notebook's bracket.

**Columns.** At L=2, $\delta t_1$ is linear and $\delta t_2$ is cubic, while $\delta t_3=0$. Split $\delta t_2(y)=\nu_1(y)A(y)+\nu_3(y-h)B(y)$ with $A,B$ linear, as at L=1. That gives six unknowns, so six columns:
$$-\langle P Q\rangle,\ -\langle xPQ\rangle,\ \langle PQ^{[2]}\rangle,\ \langle xPQ^{[2]}\rangle,\ \langle P^{[-2]}Q\rangle,\ \langle xP^{[-2]}Q\rangle,$$
with $P=Q^j$ (the ket's dual) and $Q=Q_i$ (the bra's direct). The 9 pairs $(i,j)$ give a $9\times6$ matrix. The prediction is rank 5 between different states at the same twist and rank 6 for the same state. This is tested without choosing any rows.

===== cell 16 (kind 2) =====
poles = Flatten[Table[{s, n}, {s, nL}, {n, Λ[[3]], Λ[[1]]}], 1];

## 6. Overlap formula: site-product reference factor

## 8. Identifying $R$
At L=1 (row 19), $R=(\Omega\cdot y_0)/(c_0\langle x_0|y_0\rangle\,\Phi_Q^{\rm vac}(y_0))$. The L=2 candidates, each built site by site:
- **$y_0$.** $|y_0^{(2)}\rangle=|y_0(\theta_1)\rangle\otimes|y_0(\theta_2)\rangle$. Each factor is the L=1 vector at that site: in the companion frame $y_0=t_{S,21}(\theta)\,t_{S,20}(\theta)\,t_1(\theta)^{-1}e_8$, with $t_{S,20}(\theta)=t_1(\theta-h)t_1(\theta)-t_2(\theta)$ and $t_{S,21}(\theta)=t_2(\theta+h)t_1(\theta-h)-t_3(\theta+h)$. It is mapped to the diagonal frame by $P_{\rm phys}$.
- **$\Phi_Q(y_0)$.** $\Phi_Q(y_0)=\prod_s[Q_1(\theta_s-2h)Q_2(\theta_s)-Q_2(\theta_s-2h)Q_1(\theta_s)]\,Q_1(\theta_s-2h)$, from the vacuum's direct $Q$'s.
- **$c_0$.** The coefficient of the monomials of $\Psi_Q(x_0)\Phi_Q(y_0)$ in the per-ladder $\det N$, with the $Q$'s at $\theta_s+kh$ treated as symbols. Every symbol not in that product is set to 0 first, which leaves those coefficients unchanged.
- **$\langle x_0|y_0\rangle$.** The candidate is $\langle x_0^{(2)}|y_0^{(2)}\rangle=(9h^4)^2$. It is not obvious that this is what enters at L=2: at L=1 it was really the $(y_0,x_0)$ entry of the inverse $x/y$ overlap matrix.

The cell reports $R$ divided by the prediction.

## 12. Localizing on site 2: $t^*$ and $\theta_2$-shifted transfer matrices
Section 10 localizes $\langle\Omega|$ on site 1 at $\theta_1-2h$; 11h shows the octet mixing and the singlet scale then stay undetermined. Here we look for transfer matrices whose action on $\langle\Omega|=\langle\Omega_{zw}\otimes\Omega_{zw}|$ is **site-2 local**, $\langle\Omega|\mathcal O=\langle\Omega_{zw}|\otimes\langle v|$ with $v\not\propto\Omega_{zw}$. All $t$'s again carry the ket's twist $\mathrm{diag}(z)$.

**Why $t^*$.** In $T=\mathcal L^{(1)}\mathcal L^{(2)}$ site 1 sits on the left in auxiliary space, which is why the degenerate point $\theta_1-2h$ localizes there. The inverse monodromy $T^{-1}=\mathcal L^{(2)-1}\mathcal L^{(1)-1}$ puts site 2 on the left. Its transfer matrices $t^*_a$ (twist $\mathrm{diag}(z)^{-1}$ on the left) are, up to c-number factors, ordinary $t$'s at shifted points, so the search reduces to CBR $T_\lambda$'s at $\theta_2+c\,h$ and products of $t$'s there.

**Locality test.** As in Section 10 a covector is an $8\times8$ matrix $M$ (row = site 1); site-2 local means $M=\Omega_{zw}\,v^T$. Since $t_3$ is a c-number and $\Omega_{zw}\otimes\Omega_{zw}$ is itself site-2 local, adding multiples of $t_3$ never changes locality.

specNewton[sys_,sr_,pr_] := Module[{vars=sys["variables"],eq=sys["equations"],jac,xx,vs,jv,ev,rs,delta,wp=pr+80},
jac=Transpose[Table[D[eq,var],{var,vars}]];xx=SetPrecision[vars/.sr,wp];
Do[vs=Map[Max[1,Abs[#]]&,xx];jv=N[jac/.Thread[vars->xx],wp];ev=N[eq/.Thread[vars->xx],wp];
