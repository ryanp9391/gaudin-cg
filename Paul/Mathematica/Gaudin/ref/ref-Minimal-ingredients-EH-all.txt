# Minimal ingredients: $\Omega_{zw}$, $R$, $x_0$, $T_{(1,1)}$, $T_{(2,1)}$ — and the contamination

gl(3), L=2, $[2,1,0]\otimes[2,1,0]$, Hermitian generators $EH$, new conventions. Self-contained: the first code cell clears the (shared) kernel. Everything is numeric at precision `pr` and every object is a function of the twists, so you can change $z,w,\varepsilon,\varphi,\omega$ freely.

**Conventions.**
- **Lax.** $\mathcal L^{(s)}_{ij}(u)=(u-\theta_s)\delta_{ij}+h\,E^{(s)}_{ij}$; $T(u)=\mathcal L^{(1)}(u)\mathcal L^{(2)}(u)\,M$ with the twist $M$ on the right (normally $M=\mathrm{diag}(z)$).
- **Transfer matrices.** $t_a$ = sum of principal quantum minors (rows antisymmetrized, $k$-th factor at $u-(k-1)h$). CBR fusion: $T_{(1,1)}(u)=t_2(u)$, $T_{(2,1)}(u)=t_2(u)t_1(u+h)-t_3(u+h)$.
- **Chain point** (P1 of `L2-diagonal-frame-overlap-EH.wb`): $h=1/2$, $\theta=(2/7,-3/5)$.

**What is deliberately not here: the $Q$'s.** Every Q-formula in this project computes $T_\lambda^B\,\langle\Omega|\Psi_B\rangle$ (validated against the eigenvectors in Sections 13–14 of the EH notebook). Here that number is computed directly from the eigenvector, as `Qside`. Nothing below depends on how it is obtained.

(* fresh kernel: clears Global` for every notebook sharing this kernel (their files are untouched) *)
ClearAll @@ Select[Names["Global`*"], FreeQ[Attributes[#], Protected] &];
(* GT basis and gl(3) generators, verbatim from the clean L=1 notebook *)
ps[rep_] := ps[rep] = Block[{res = (Prepend[rep, #1] &) /@ Tuples[Table[Range[rep[[1, i + 1]], rep[[1, i]]], {i, 1, Length[rep[[1]]] - 1}]]}, If[Length[res[[1, 1]]] == 1, Reverse[Sort[res]], Reverse[Sort[Flatten[ps /@ res, 1]]]]];
dim[rep_] := dim[rep] = Length[ps[{rep}]];
J0[k_][pat_] := J0[k][pat] = Sum[pat[[k, i]], {i, 1, k}] - Sum[pat[[k - 1, i]], {i, 1, k - 1}];
Jp[k_][pat_] := Jp[k][pat] = -Sum[(Product[pat[[k, i]] - i - pat[[k + 1, j]] + j, {j, Range[k + 1]}]/Product[pat[[k, i]] - i - pat[[k, j]] + j, {j, Complement[Range[k], {i}]}])*v[ReplacePart[pat, {k, i} -> pat[[k, i]] + 1]], {i, 1, k}];
Jm[k_][pat_] := Jm[k][pat] = Sum[(Product[pat[[k, i]] - i - pat[[k - 1, j]] + j, {j, Range[k - 1]}]/Product[pat[[k, i]] - i - pat[[k, j]] + j, {j, Complement[Range[k], {i}]}])*v[ReplacePart[pat, {k, i} -> pat[[k, i]] - 1]], {i, 1, k}];
JJ[k_, l_, rep_] := JJ[k, l, rep] = Which[
  k == l, DiagonalMatrix[J0[k] /@ ps[{rep}]],
  l == k + 1, Transpose[Table[(Coefficient[Jp[k][ps[{rep}][[i]]], v[#1]] &) /@ ps[{rep}], {i, 1, Length[ps[{rep}]]}]],
  l == k - 1, Transpose[Table[(Coefficient[Jm[l][ps[{rep}][[i]]], v[#1]] &) /@ ps[{rep}], {i, 1, Length[ps[{rep}]]}]],
  l > k + 1, JJ[k, k + 1, rep] . JJ[k + 1, l, rep] - JJ[k + 1, l, rep] . JJ[k, k + 1, rep],
  l < k - 1, JJ[k, k - 1, rep] . JJ[k - 1, l, rep] - JJ[k - 1, l, rep] . JJ[k, k - 1, rep]];
(* rep, chain point (P1 of the EH notebook), working precision *)
Λ = {2, 1, 0}; d1 = dim[Λ]; d2 = d1^2; id1 = IdentityMatrix[d1];
hN = 1/2; thN = {2/7, -3/5}; θ12 = thN[[1]] - thN[[2]]; pr = 80;
(* Hermitian generators EH = Rd.E.Rd^-1 with EH^T = EH_ji. Rd is diagonal and unrelated to the rotation R of Section 2 *)
MsolEH = Module[{Mv = Array[mm, d1]}, Mv /. First[Solve[Join[Thread[DeleteCases[Union[Flatten[Table[DiagonalMatrix[Mv] . JJ[j, i, Λ] - Transpose[JJ[i, j, Λ]] . DiagonalMatrix[Mv], {i, 3}, {j, 3}]]], 0] == 0], {mm[1] == 1}], Mv]]];
Rd = Sqrt[MsolEH];
Ee[i_, j_] := Ee[i, j] = DiagonalMatrix[Rd] . JJ[i, j, Λ] . DiagonalMatrix[1/Rd];
EN[i_, j_] := EN[i, j] = N[Ee[i, j], pr];
EtotN[i_, j_] := EtotN[i, j] = KroneckerProduct[EN[i, j], id1] + KroneckerProduct[id1, EN[i, j]];
(* exact entries carry surds that Mathematica does not combine canonically, so === {0} can miss true zeros *)
zeroQ[e_] := AllTrue[Union[Flatten[{e}]], PossibleZeroQ];
(* total weight of each two-site basis state; KroneckerProduct order, site 1 is the slow index *)
wtBasis = Flatten[Table[Table[J0[k][p1] + J0[k][p2], {k, 3}], {p1, ps[{Λ}]}, {p2, ps[{Λ}]}], 1];
secOf[μ_] := Flatten[Position[wtBasis, μ]];
kron[a_, b_] := Flatten[KroneckerProduct[a, b]];
e1 = UnitVector[d1, 1];
(* exp of a nilpotent matrix as a finite sum starting from the identity (MatrixPower[nilpotent, 0] errors) *)
nexp[A_] := id1 + Sum[MatrixPower[A, k]/k!, {k, 1, 4}];
Grid[{{"one-site, two-site dimension", {d1, d2}}, {"Rd", Rd}, {"EH^T = EH_ji", And @@ Flatten[Table[zeroQ[Transpose[Ee[i, j]] - Ee[j, i]], {i, 3}, {j, 3}]]}, {"weight sectors", Length[Union[wtBasis]]}}, Frame -> All, Alignment -> Left]

## 1. Transfer matrices
`tL2[a, u, M]` is the two-site $t_a(u)$ with twist $M$; `tL1[s, a, u, M]` is the one-site $t_a(u)$ of site $s$ alone (needed for the localized covectors). `T11`, `T21` (two-site) and `T11L1`, `T21L1` (one-site) are the fused $T_{(1,1)}$, $T_{(2,1)}$. Checks: quantum determinant $t_3=\chi_3\,\nu_1(u)\nu_2(u-h)\nu_3(u-2h)$ with $\nu_n(x)=\prod_s(x-\theta_s+h\lambda_n)$, commutativity, weight conservation.

(* one-site Lax at site s, as a 3x3 array of d1 x d1 blocks *)
lax[s_, x_] := Table[KroneckerDelta[i, j] (x - thN[[s]]) id1 + hN EN[i, j], {i, 3}, {j, 3}];
twistR[T_, M_] := Table[Sum[T[[i, l]] M[[l, j]], {l, 3}], {i, 3}, {j, 3}];
mono1[s_, M_][x_] := twistR[lax[s, x], M];
(* two-site monodromy: E^(1) = E⊗1 and E^(2) = 1⊗E, so (L^(1) L^(2))_ij = Σ_k L1_ik ⊗ L2_kj *)
mono2[M_][x_] := With[{L1 = lax[1, x], L2 = lax[2, x]}, twistR[Table[Sum[KroneckerProduct[L1[[i, k]], L2[[k, j]]], {k, 3}], {i, 3}, {j, 3}], M]];
(* t_a: principal quantum minors, rows antisymmetrized, k-th factor at x-(k-1)h *)
tq[mono_, a_, x_] := Sum[Sum[Signature[σ] Dot @@ Table[mono[x - (k - 1) hN][[σ[[k]], rows[[k]]]], {k, a}], {σ, Permutations[rows]}], {rows, Subsets[Range[3], {a}]}];
tL2[a_, x_, M_] := tq[mono2[M], a, x];
tL1[s_, a_, x_, M_] := tq[mono1[s, M], a, x];
T11[x_, M_] := tL2[2, x, M];
T21[x_, M_] := tL2[2, x, M] . tL2[1, x + hN, M] - tL2[3, x + hN, M];
T11L1[s_, x_, M_] := tL1[s, 2, x, M];
T21L1[s_, x_, M_] := tL1[s, 2, x, M] . tL1[s, 1, x + hN, M] - tL1[s, 3, x + hN, M];
nu[n_][x_] := Product[x - thN[[s]] + hN Λ[[n]], {s, 2}];
Zt = DiagonalMatrix[N[{3/2, 5/4, 2/3}, pr]];

    Max[Abs[With[{A = tL2[1, 1/3, Zt], B = tL2[2, -2/5, Zt]}, A . B - B . A]]],

## 2. $\Omega_{zw}$, $R$ and $x_0$
- **$R$ and $\Omega_{zw}$.** $R=e^{r_1E_{12}}e^{r_2E_{13}}e^{r_3E_{23}}$ (raising operators), $\Omega_{zw}=e_1^TR$, two-site bra $\Omega=\Omega_{zw}\otimes\Omega_{zw}=(e_1\otimes e_1)^T(R\otimes R)$. The $r_i(z,w)$ make $\Omega$ the vacuum of the $w$ companion twist written in the frame where $z$ is diagonal, $G'=P_{\rm aux}G(w)P_{\rm aux}^{-1}$. $w_3$ drops out.
- **$x_0$.** The SoV covector $e_1$ of the companion frame, carried to the diagonal frame: $x_0=(e_1^TP_{\rm phys}^{-1})^{\otimes2}$. It is used only for the normalization $\langle x_0|\Psi\rangle=1$ that the Q-formulas use.
- **Checks** at a finite twist: $P_{\rm aux}G(z)P_{\rm aux}^{-1}=\mathrm{diag}(z)$; $\Omega$ is a left eigenvector of every $t_a(G')$; $\langle x_0|e_1e_1\rangle=(z_2-z_1)^2$.

===== cell 6 (kind 2) =====
rOm[zv_, wv_] := {(wv[[1]] - zv[[1]])/(wv[[1]] - zv[[2]]),
   ((wv[[1]] - zv[[1]]) (zv[[1]] - wv[[2]]) (zv[[2]] - zv[[3]]))/((zv[[1]] - zv[[2]]) (wv[[1]] - zv[[3]]) (wv[[2]] - zv[[3]])),
   ((wv[[1]] - zv[[2]]) (zv[[2]] - wv[[2]]) (zv[[1]] - zv[[3]]))/((zv[[1]] - zv[[2]]) (wv[[1]] - zv[[3]]) (zv[[3]] - wv[[2]]))};
Rrot[zv_, wv_] := With[{r = rOm[zv, wv]}, nexp[r[[1]] EN[1, 2]] . nexp[r[[2]] EN[1, 3]] . nexp[r[[3]] EN[2, 3]]];
RrotInv[zv_, wv_] := With[{r = rOm[zv, wv]}, nexp[-r[[3]] EN[2, 3]] . nexp[-r[[2]] EN[1, 3]] . nexp[-r[[1]] EN[1, 2]]];
RR[zv_, wv_] := With[{g = Rrot[zv, wv]}, KroneckerProduct[g, g]];
Om1[zv_, wv_] := e1 . Rrot[zv, wv];
Om2[zv_, wv_] := With[{o = Om1[zv, wv]}, kron[o, o]];
(* frame changes, verbatim from the clean L=1 notebook: P_aux G(z) P_aux^-1 = diag(z) in aux space, P_phys its physical-space twin *)
ee[i_, j_] := Table[KroneckerDelta[i, ii] KroneckerDelta[j, jj], {ii, 3}, {jj, 3}];
Gc[tw_] := {{Total[tw], -(tw[[1]] tw[[2]] + tw[[1]] tw[[3]] + tw[[2]] tw[[3]]), Times @@ tw}, {1, 0, 0}, {0, 1, 0}};
rsSol[zv_] := {s1 -> zv[[1]]^2/((zv[[1]] - zv[[2]]) (zv[[1]] - zv[[3]])), s2 -> -(zv[[2]]/(zv[[1]] (zv[[2]] - zv[[3]]))), s3 -> 1/(zv[[1]] zv[[2]]), ρ1 -> -(zv[[3]]/zv[[2]]), ρ2 -> -(zv[[3]]/zv[[1]]), ρ3 -> (zv[[2]]^2 (zv[[3]] - zv[[1]]))/(zv[[1]]^2 (zv[[2]] - zv[[3]]))};
Paux[zv_] := (MatrixExp[-ρ1 ee[2, 3]] . MatrixExp[-ρ2 ee[1, 3]] . MatrixExp[-ρ3 ee[1, 2]] . DiagonalMatrix[1/{s1, s2, s3}] . MatrixExp[-(1/zv[[1]]) ee[2, 1]] . MatrixExp[(1/(zv[[1]] zv[[2]])) ee[3, 1]] . MatrixExp[-((zv[[1]] + zv[[2]])/(zv[[1]] zv[[2]])) ee[3, 2]]) /. rsSol[zv];
Pphys[zv_] := With[{q = rsSol[zv]}, nexp[(ρ1 /. q) EN[3, 2]] . nexp[(ρ2 /. q) EN[3, 1]] . nexp[(ρ3 /. q) EN[2, 1]] . DiagonalMatrix[Table[Product[({s1, s2, s3}[[k]] /. q)^J0[k][p], {k, 3}], {p, ps[{Λ}]}]] . nexp[(1/zv[[1]]) EN[1, 2]] . nexp[-(1/(zv[[1]] zv[[2]])) EN[1, 3]] . nexp[((zv[[1]] + zv[[2]])/(zv[[1]] zv[[2]])) EN[2, 3]]];

zT = N[{3/2, 5/4, 2/3}, pr]; wT = N[{5/2, 7/3, 3/5}, pr];

## 3. Eigenstates at small twist and the highest-weight states
$z=e^{\varepsilon\varphi}$, $w=e^{\varepsilon\omega}$. In each weight sector that contains a highest-weight (HW) state, diagonalize $t_1+\pi t_2$ at $u=3/7$ and normalize $\langle x_0|\Psi\rangle=1$ (so $\|\Psi\|\sim\varepsilon^{-k}$). $P_{\rm HW}(\mu)$ is the orthogonal projector onto the untwisted HW vectors of weight $\mu$ (killed by the total $E_{12}$ and $E_{23}$). The **HW-limit states** are those with $\|P_{\rm HW}\Psi\|/\|\Psi\|\to1$; there should be six: $[4,2,0],[4,1,1],[3,3,0],[3,2,1]_{1,2},[2,2,2]$. The two $[3,2,1]$ copies are ordered by the eigenvalue of $t_1+\pi t_2$.

## 4. Localization: $T_\lambda(\theta_1-2h)$ on site 1, two $t$'s on site 2

## 5. Undoing $R$: the chain, and the signal/contamination split
Since $\langle\Omega|=(e_1\otimes e_1)^T(R\otimes R)$, site 1's localization can be rewritten as $\langle\Omega|T_\lambda(\theta_1-2h)=f_\lambda\,c_\lambda\,(R\otimes R)$ with
$$c_\lambda=\langle v_\lambda|R^{-1}\otimes e_1^T,\qquad \langle v_\lambda|R^{-1}=e_1^T\,T_\lambda^{L=1}[g^{-T}\mathrm{diag}(z)g^T](\theta_1-2h)\ (g=R\text{ in the fundamental}).$$
For an eigenstate, the **chain** is
$$c_\lambda\cdot(R\otimes R)\Psi_B=\frac{T_\lambda^B\,\langle\Omega|\Psi_B\rangle}{f_\lambda}=:\texttt{Qside}.$$
Raising operators kill HW vectors, so $(R\otimes R)P_{\rm HW}\Psi=P_{\rm HW}\Psi$ **exactly**. That splits Qside, at any $\varepsilon$, into
$$\texttt{Qside}=\underbrace{c_\lambda\cdot P_{\rm HW}\Psi}_{\text{signal}\ \to\ \varepsilon^{-k}\times\text{CG}}+\underbrace{c_\lambda\cdot(R\otimes R)(1-P_{\rm HW})\Psi}_{\text{contamination}},\qquad \kappa=\texttt{Qside}/\text{signal}.$$
The naive hope was $\kappa\to1$. The table gives $\kappa$ at two values of $\varepsilon$: if they agree, the contamination is of the same order as the signal. Also included is $C=c_{(2,1)}+h z_3c_{(1,1)}$ (Section 11g of the EH notebook), whose top-weight component vanishes. "CG = 0" marks an exactly vanishing CG number; then the column shows Qside/(‖c‖‖Ψ‖) instead of $\kappa$.

## 6. Where the contamination comes from, weight by weight
The vector $(R\otimes R)\Psi_\perp$, with $\Psi_\perp=(1-P_{\rm HW})\Psi$, has components only at weights $\nu\ge\mu$, because $R$ raises. So
$$\text{contamination}=\sum_{\nu\ge\mu}c_\lambda^{(\nu)}\cdot\big[(R\otimes R)\Psi_\perp\big]_\nu .$$
`showContam[μ, copy, λ]` lists every nonzero term. For each it gives the $\varepsilon$-power of $\|c^{(\nu)}\|$ and of $\|[(R\otimes R)\Psi_\perp]_\nu\|$ (read off from the two values of $\varepsilon$), and the term divided by the signal. The last rows give the $\varepsilon$-powers of the signal and of $\|\Psi\|$. Use it only where the signal is nonzero, i.e. not for $(1,1)$ at $[4,1,1]$ or $[2,2,2]$.

**What to look for.** At $\nu=[4,2,0]$, $c^{(\nu)}$ is $O(1)$, because at $z=1$ the twist is trivial and $c_\lambda\propto e_1\otimes e_1$. The signal's $c^{(\mu)}$ is suppressed by powers of $\varepsilon$, and $[(R\otimes R)\Psi_\perp]_{420}$ is one order below $\|\Psi\|$. The two products come out at the same order.

## 7. Cancelling the contamination with $a+b\,T_{(1,1)}+c\,T_{(2,1)}$
In the frame with $R$ undone, the bare row $\langle\Omega|\Psi\rangle$ has covector $e_1\otimes e_1$, which is pure top weight, because $\langle\Omega|=(e_1\otimes e_1)^T(R\otimes R)$. So the combination
$$Q_v=a\,\langle\Omega|\Psi\rangle+b\,\texttt{Qside}_{(1,1)}+c\,\texttt{Qside}_{(2,1)}=\langle\Omega|\Psi\rangle\Big[a+b\,\frac{T^B_{(1,1)}}{f_{(1,1)}}+c\,\frac{T^B_{(2,1)}}{f_{(2,1)}}\Big]$$
has covector $c_v=a\,e_1e_1+b\,c_{(1,1)}+c\,c_{(2,1)}$, and everything from Section 5 is linear in $v=(a,b,c)$.
- **Step 1: kill the top weight with $a$.** Set $a=-(b\,c_{(1,1)}+c\,c_{(2,1)})\cdot e_1e_1=-3h^2z_2z_3\,(b-h z_3\,c)$, using the $[4,2,0]$ row of the Section 10d table. `vTop[{b,c}, d]` builds this $v$. Then the table gives $\kappa$ for $(b,c)=(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$.
- **Step 2: the singlet.** It also has contamination from intermediate weights. For the top-killed basis covectors, $K_b$ and $K_c$ are the contaminations and $S$ the signal of $c_{(2,1)}$; the signal of $c_{(1,1)}$ on the singlet is exactly 0. The ratio $\beta=b/c=-K_c/K_b$ cancels the singlet's contamination.

### Results of Section 7 (2026-10-02)
- **Killing the top weight with $a$ is enough for every state except the singlet.** With $a=-3h^2z_2z_3(b-hz_3c)$, both $(b,c)=(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$ give $\kappa\to1$ (deviation $O(\varepsilon)$) for $[4,1,1]$ (only $(2,1)$; on $(1,1)$ both sides vanish), $[3,3,0]$, and both octets. Section 11g's $C$ is the special case $a=0$, which is why $C$ vanished on $[3,3,0]$.
- **Octet mixing is fixed.** The two top-killed covectors have rank 2 on the octet HW space, so both octet HW states are determined up to scale by clean leading-order data.
- **Singlet.** With the top weight killed, it still has intermediate-weight contamination. In units of the $(2,1)$ signal, it is $K_b/S=-(t-1)/(3h)$ for $c_{(1,1)}$ and $K_c/S=(2t-3)/3$ for $c_{(2,1)}$, with $t=\theta_{12}/h$. It cancels for
$$\beta=b/c=h\,\frac{2t-3}{t-1}.$$
  The forms were recognized at P1 ($-18/35$, $19/105$, $19/54$) and **validated at P2** ($-14/15$, $13/45$, $13/42$ predicted and observed). They are independent of $\varphi$ and $\omega$ (three direction pairs).
- **One universal combination.** $v^*=(a,\beta,1)$, i.e.
$$\langle\Omega|\Psi_B\rangle\Big[-3h^2z_2z_3(\beta-hz_3)+\beta\,\frac{T^B_{(1,1)}(\theta_1-2h)}{f_{(1,1)}}+\frac{T^B_{(2,1)}(\theta_1-2h)}{f_{(2,1)}}\Big]=c_{v^*}\cdot h_\mu\,\varepsilon^{-k}\,(1+O(\varepsilon)),$$
  gives $\kappa\to1$ for all five non-top HW states at P1 and P2. $[4,2,0]$ is clean with any single $T_\lambda$.
- **Scope.** Only $[2,1,0]^2$, two chain points, $\varepsilon\le10^{-4}$, 80 digits. The $\beta(t)$ formula rests on two points. It is singular at $t=1$, where $f_\lambda$ vanishes anyway.
