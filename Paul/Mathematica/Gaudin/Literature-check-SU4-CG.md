## 0. Bottom line

**Is there an SU(4) / SU(N >= 4) analogue of the SU(3) Draayer-Akiyama / ndsu3lib lineage? No.** What exists is a handful of general-N codes that all descend from, or are compared with, the Munich code (Alex-Kalus-Huckleberry-von Delft 2011), a few SU(4)-specific programs and printed tables for small irreps, and an old, mostly analytic, nuclear-physics literature on SU(4) > SU(2)xSU(2). Nothing I found uses a slice / string-condition reduction as a numerical engine for N >= 4, and nothing I found is faster than Munich for SU(4) highest-weight (HW) vectors. One thing the notes do not mention: the Munich group itself wrote a Weyl-chamber version of its algorithm (Everding, LMU bachelor thesis 2011; APS March Meeting 2012 abstract) claimed to accelerate the calculation "by a factor of order N!". Read in the thesis, the gain is in the descendants; the HW null space (the step the notes' Table 4 compares against) is the same full null space; the code is not on the group's web page and I found no paper.

**Is the missing-label triple known?** The count (3 for SU(4)) is standard. What is a *theorem* in the literature is that polynomial invariants of the product resolve the multiplicity for U(N) and for any compact group (Klink-Ton-That 1996; Gliske-Klink-Ton-That 2005; an SU(4) example in Klink-Ton-That-Wills 1993), so "labelling operators exist and suffice" is not new. What I did not find anywhere is the specific statement: one cubic plus two quartic Laurent coefficients of the two-site Gaudin cdet, t-independent, commuting, with simple joint spectrum, as the three SU(4) missing labels; nor the failure of the cubic alone, nor the exchange-parity forcing. Two negative statements in the notes are, however, *known*: naive trace Hamiltonians fail to commute from degree 4 on (Chervov-Rybnikov-Talalaev 2004, Talalaev 2004), and the "higher rank" missing-label problem was explicitly called open by Crampe-Poulain d'Andecy-Vinet (2021/23).

**Is the Weyl/BGG identity known?** Not found. The surrounding structure (Gaudin algebra on tensor products with Verma modules, dot-action twisted exponents, dynamical Weyl group = restriction of Verma intertwiners, slice parametrisation of singular vectors) is in the literature (Frenkel 2005 section 5; Etingof-Varchenko 2002; Styrkas-Tarasov-Varchenko 2004; Mudrov 2019/22) but none of it states (12) or (18). A category-O argument sketched in section 5 `[INF]` suggests that (18) is provable (the "one unproved step" of notes section 7.2 is exactness of Hom(M(mu), -), which holds for dominant integral mu because M(mu) is projective), and that the commutation on the whole weight space for every lambda' (notes section 5.4, item 2) follows from Talalaev's theorem by Zariski density.

### The three pre-emption questions

* **(i) the engine:** *not pre-empted.* Partial precedents: Mudrov (slice + extremal projector as a criterion, quantum groups), SU(3) HW-first chains (yesterday), Gliske et al. (HW vector mapped by differential operators), Dang et al. (U(4) null space). No numerical level recursion with string and dangling conditions for N >= 4.
* **(ii) the triple:** *the concept is pre-empted, the specific triple is not found.* Klink-Ton-That: polynomial invariants (generalized Casimirs) always resolve the multiplicity (theorem), with "binary coupling" operators that the authors say "do not work in general". The Gaudin labels for SU(N >= 4) with simple joint spectrum, and the failure of the cubic alone, were not found.
* **(iii) the Weyl identity:** *not pre-empted, not found.*

**Recommended framing (short; details in section 10).** (1) Replace "the SU(4) literature was not searched" by the results of this check. (3) Benchmark claim: "general-N null-space code (Munich as distributed), HW step only; a Weyl-chamber variant exists that accelerates descendants". (4) Cite Talalaev/CRT for the quantum correction at degree 4, MTV Theorem 2.1 for the polynomial-kernel opers, Frenkel/EV/STV/Mudrov for the Verma/dynamical-Weyl structure.

## 2. K1: numerical SU(4) / SU(N) CGC codes (the evidence behind the table)

**Verdict: PARTIALLY KNOWN (landscape found, benchmark risk assessed); the engine is not pre-empted.** Confidence 80%: I could not read the CGSU4 paper, Gliske-Klink-Ton-That 2007, or Klink-Ton-That-Wills 1993, and I cannot rule out unpublished codes inside nuclear-physics groups (the 2024 LSU C++ library is one such).

### 2.1 What the Munich paper says, and what it does not say `[FT]`

* *Framing.* The introduction says explicit CGC formulas exist "for certain special cases, e.g. where S' is the defining representation" (Vilenkin-Klimyk), that symbolic packages such as LiE "also allow the computation of certain CGCs" but were "conceived as general-purpose software ... rather than a high-speed implementation", and that "previous attempts ... are either not sufficiently general for our purposes (Williams-Wynne 1994; Rowe-Repka, J. Math. Phys. 38 (1997) 4363), or require mathematical methods (Gliske-Klink-Ton-That, Acta Appl. Math. 95 (2007) 51) much more advanced than ours". So the Munich authors knew of the Klink school and, in their own words, found its methods more advanced than they wanted.
* *Algorithm.* Section X: HW state ansatz (35), restricted to product states whose weights add up to the target weight; the raising operators give the homogeneous system (36), with N^{S''} independent solutions; the outer-multiplicity ambiguity is resolved by "a convention, suggested by G. Zarand (private communication, 2009)": reduced row echelon form of the solution matrix, then Gram-Schmidt of the rows from top to bottom. Section XI: descendants, all states of a given weight at once, from an overdetermined but consistent system (40). Section XII: the three steps (Littlewood-Richardson, HW CGCs, lowering).
* *Performance statement (the only one).* "The speed of the algorithm depends polynomially on the dimensions of the irreps ... smaller su(3) cases (e.g. dim S = 6, dim S' = 15) run instantly, while medium-sized su(5) cases (e.g. dim S = 35, dim S' = 224) take a few minutes, and larger su(5) cases (e.g. dim S = 280, dim S' = 420) require several hours" on a 2 GHz CPU. **No SU(4) timing, no exponent, no statement about which step dominates.** Hence: (a) the notes' sentence in section 1, "the largest case of the SU(4) sweep of the Munich code of [1]", refers to *our* sweep S = 1..8 of the distributed code, not to anything in the Munich paper; (b) the O(n_W^3) cost and its attribution to a dense SVD are this repository's profile (README, notes section 2.2), which is fair but should not be attributed to the authors.
* *Outlook (their words):* "it should be possible to greatly speed up our algorithm by exploiting ... the Weyl group ... Work along these lines is currently in progress." This became the Everding thesis (next item).

### 2.2 The Munich group's own Weyl-chamber version `[FT]` thesis, `[AB]` talk

* Everding, "Calculation of Clebsch-Gordan coefficients via Weyl group symmetry", Bachelorarbeit, Fakultaet fuer Physik, LMU Muenchen, 12 August 2011 (referee J. von Delft). Aim (section 1.1): "to write a C++ library ... However, the code described here uses an improved algorithm that accelerates the calculation by a factor of order N!. To achieve this, it exploits ... Weyl-group symmetry." Outline (section 3.1): (1) HW CGCs, (2) lowering in the dominant Weyl chamber, (3) find the permutations ("connectors") for each state, (4) map the dominant-chamber states and coefficients to all other chambers.
* **The HW step is not accelerated.** Section 3.3: the raising conditions are imposed on "every possible combination of a state in the dominant Weyl chamber with a permutation", and "to solve the system we determine the nullspace of the matrix. The amount of linearly independent solutions is equal to the outer multiplicity". So for the benchmark of the notes (HW solve only) the Weyl variant would give the same n_W-sized null space. The gain "of order N!" is in the descendants.
* Outlook (section 5): own dense C++ matrix class, a sparse class "could" give a further speed-up; "with this new algorithm it is possible to compute CGCs even for very large SU(N) (like SU(20)) efficiently. In contrast, the old program is not capable of handling SU(N) of that order"; "the publication of a paper ... is planned and we may also install a web interface ... that uses the new algorithm". No timings anywhere in the thesis.
* APS March Meeting 2012, W26.11 (post-deadline session; Alex, Everding, Littelmann, von Delft; `[AB]`, fetched from meetings.aps.org): "Further exploitation of the Weyl symmetry of SU(N) irreps leads to a significant speed-up compared to our previous algorithm ... we provide a well-tested computer code for download and online use."
* **Availability: not found.** The group's CGC page (fetched 2026-10-22, `[FT]`) still offers only `ClebschGordan.cpp` (the 2010 code) and `Alex2010.pdf`. Searches: arXiv API (author and title queries), Crossref bibliographic queries, four web queries: no journal paper by Everding / Alex / Littelmann / von Delft on the Weyl variant. So "Munich as distributed" is correct, and the notes' sentence should say that a Weyl-chamber variant exists and accelerates the descendants (section 10).

### 2.3 Ports and users of the Munich algorithm

* **SUNRepresentations.jl** `[FT]` README: "Compute Clebsch-Gordan coefficients for general SU(N) groups. Reimplementation of arXiv:1009.0437. Compatibility with TensorKit.jl." "As computing the CGCs is a relatively expensive operation, this package automatically caches the results"; `precompute_disk_cache(N, a_max)` precomputes all products with Dynkin labels below `a_max`. Anyone doing non-abelian SU(N) tensor networks in the TensorKit ecosystem therefore uses the Munich algorithm. The TensorKit.jl paper (Devos-Haegeman, arXiv:2508.10076, `[FT]` grep) contains no SU(N)-CGC statement.
* **pyclebsch** (Balaji et al., arXiv:2509.25865, `[FT]` section 2.3): "Our algorithm, described in [15], is based on the algorithm of [Alex et al.]. We have extended it ... based on the algorithms of [LiE, GroupMath] as well as [Hermitian Young operators]." The extension: CGC bases of repeated irreps are symmetrised under the symmetric group permuting the repeated factors, which reduces the number of non-zero magnetic matrix elements. This is the SU(3) lattice-gauge community's answer to the multiplicity problem; for two equal factors it is exactly the exchange-parity decomposition of the notes' section 4.3 (see section 3.8 below).
* **Dang et al.** (arXiv:2405.06843 for U(3), arXiv:2407.09665 for U(4); `[FT]` U(4)): the canonical-chain coupling coefficients follow "the methodologies for U(N) proffered by Alex et al."; HW CGCs "simultaneously for all alpha by solving the null space of the matrix P(HW)"; "the CGCs no longer uniquely exist and are contingent upon the numerical method ... the solutions based on different numerical methods are equivalent and can be realized from one another by a unitary or orthogonal transformation. ... one only needs to ensure that the first non-vanishing entry belonging to each vector is always positive". The paper's literature survey (Introduction): SU(3): "a Fortran package was published more than 50 years ago [Akiyama-Draayer, Draayer-Akiyama], then converted into C++ [SU3lib] using the same algorithm but boosted precision"; U(4) side: "Hecht and Pang first attempted ... in 1969, then Draayer independently ... transformation between the canonical and physical bases. Partensky and Maguin, and Rowe and Repka give explicit expressions for some specific coefficients. Nevertheless, none of these provided a simple, yet rigorous procedure for the computation of arbitrary coupling and recoupling coefficients of U_ST(4)." Data availability: "an early version of a C++ library ... a fully streamlined U(4) package remains under development ... the authors are prepared to entertain special requests". **Conclusion: as of mid-2024 the nuclear SU(4) community had no public general SU(4)xSU(4) code and adopted the Munich null-space approach.** This supports benchmarking against Munich and is evidence that a faster HW engine would be of use there.
* **kercl/sun** `[AB]`: C library implementing the Munich algorithm; Clebsch-Gordan listed as planned, none implemented.

### 2.4 QSpace (Weichselbaum) `[FT]`

* Annals of Physics 327 (2012) 2972, appendix B: "In order to determine the decomposition into IREPs, a tempting route may be through the construction of the group's Casimir operators ... and their simultaneous diagonalization together with the z-operators. However, in the presence of outer or inner multiplicity, subspaces exist that are fully degenerate in Casimir operators as well as in the z-operators. ... In practice, therefore, a more straightforward approach has been adopted": seed state = first product basis vector with a new component, raising operators until a (unique) maximum-weight state, multiplet by lowering, repeat. Deterministic, but the multiplicity basis depends on the order of the Cartesian product basis. In the NRG applications of that paper (multiplets of dimension at most 13 for Sp(6), small SU(3) multiplets) the CGC generation was "already sufficiently fast"; the Weyl symmetries "may be used to evaluate or encode CGC spaces more efficiently" (citing the Munich paper) but were not exploited (so the Weyl idea was in the air in 2012, consistent with section 2.2).
* SciPost Phys. Codebases 40 (2024): "The process of tensor product decomposition is fully numerical ... The generalized raising/lowering operators are also computed fully numerically ... [they] could be generated directly, e.g. based on Gelfand-Tsetlin pattern calculus for SU(N) [Alex et al.]; but with general non-abelian symmetries also beyond SU(N) in mind, this approach is not adopted". CGTs are stored in MPFR 128-bit (quad) precision; "for SU(4) CGT coefficients as small as 1e-9 have been encountered" (so sparsity thresholds need high precision). On multiplicity (section 2.10): "OM can become arbitrarily large ... Based on the unboundedness of OM even for rank-3 CGTs, there appears no simple way to fix a canonical OM basis ... To the best of our knowledge, there is no superior systematic way to deal with arbitrarily large OM for general non-abelian symmetries. Hence a bottom-up constructive approach is adopted ... arbitrary but fixed, at the expense of a history dependent RC_STORE." Initialisation (appendix): "very fast for small symmetries such as seconds for SU(2) ... for rank-2 symmetries like SU(3) ... within minutes. But for SU(4) one should be already prepared to wait several hours while the RC_STORE is built the first time" (the store covers all small irreps and their products up to a threshold, so this is not a single-product timing).
* Phys. Rev. Research 2 (2020) 023385 (X-symbols; `[FT]`): "contractions of CGTs were not tabulated due to the presence of outer multiplicity ... the prescription [of the 2012 paper] was to always recontract all CGTs based on their particular instantiation in OM space. However, from a practical point of view, this led to significant computational overhead for larger non-abelian symmetries, such as SU(N >= 4)"; the OM basis of a CGT is "history dependent, adding OM components as they occur". On the cost of fusing two large multiplets: computing a 1j-symbol by decomposing (q qbar) "is not practical for very large multiplets ... one typically fuses a given large effective state space with new local state spaces of small dimension ... for most part, one can avoid fusing two large multiplets. This is specifically important for large symmetries such as SU(N >= 4)". So the regime of the notes (two large SU(4) irreps) is one the tensor-network community deliberately avoids because the decomposition of a |q|^2-dimensional space is expensive: a motivation for a fast HW engine.
* **Relevance.** (a) QSpace is an independent general-N CGC generator with no published SU(4) benchmark of the kind we do. (b) The authors state that no canonical OM basis is known to them: this is the exact gap the Gaudin triple fills, and it is a quotable motivation; it is also a warning that a "canonical" Gaudin basis will be compared with arbitrary-but-fixed bases whose changes of basis are unitary but not diagonal.

### 2.5 The Klink school, the VCS school, complementary groups, projection operators

* **Gliske thesis** (Iowa, 9 May 2006; `[FT]`, Wayback copy of the PDF) and **Gliske-Klink-Ton-That**, Acta Appl. Math. 88 (2005) 229 (`[AB]`: "The main theorem of this paper shows that the eigenvalues of generalized Casimir operators are always sufficient to break the multiplicity. Using this theorem algorithms are given for computing the overlap between different sets of eigenvalues of commuting generalized Casimir operators, which are the generalized Racah coefficients"), and Acta Appl. Math. 95 (2007) 51-72 (`[AB]`: "we use the Gelfand-Zetlin basis ... Our first result is to derive algorithms for generating Gelfand-Zetlin bases from Gelfand-Zetlin tableaux. Given these concrete basis realizations we develop algorithms for computing the Clebsch-Gordan coefficients themselves"). The thesis algorithm: realise V(M) and the product as polynomials in the entries of a matrix Z with n = p_1 + ... + p_r rows (Bargmann-Fock); the HW vector f_max of V(M) is mapped into the product space by products of differential operators L_{ij}; the Borel conditions L_{12}, L_{34}, ... give a matrix B; "the number of linearly independent maps gives the multiplicity"; multiplicity labels are eigenvalues of mutually commuting generalized Casimir operators (below); the GT basis is made concrete by the same method; Clebsch-Gordan coefficients are then differential inner products. Cost: "3-fold SU(3) examples can be computed within a few minutes, and 4-fold SU(3) examples in a matter of hours" in Maple; worked example SU(3), [2,1,0]^{x3}: spanning set of dimension 168, matrix 8 x 168, "two to three minutes total" on a desktop. The abstract promises SU(2), SU(3), SU(4) examples; the body I read has SU(2) and SU(3) only. This is a *HW-first* (highest-weight vector of the irrep mapped into the product space) algorithm, different from both Munich and the slice engine, with n_W-independent costs but a large symbolic spanning set. Its symbolic precursor is Klink-Ton-That, J. Comput. Phys. 80 (1989) 453-471, "Calculation of Clebsch-Gordan and Racah coefficients using symbolic manipulation programs" (`[CR]` title only).
* **Rowe-Repka** (Found. * **Pan-Draayer I** (J. Math. ### 2.9 Verdict on the benchmark risk

1. **Faster than Munich for SU(4) HW vectors or full tables?** Nothing found, and nobody claims it for SU(4). The accelerations that are claimed: the Weyl-chamber variant (descendants, "order N!", unreleased), pyclebsch (fewer non-zero matrix elements, not time), the SU(3)-specific codes (N = 3 only).
2. **Pre-empts the slice engine for N >= 4?** No (section 4).
3. **Two sentences of the notes are inaccurate:** "the Munich code is the only general-N code we know" (section 1) and "the largest case of the SU(4) sweep of the Munich code of [1]". The accurate statement: Munich is the only general-N GT-basis code *currently distributed by its authors* (the Iowa Maple package is no longer online) and the origin of the other public ones; the SU(4) sweep is ours (section 9, section 10).
4. **Missing comparison that would be most informative:** CGSU4 (SU(4)-specific, general irreps) if its files can be obtained; a same-machine Munich run with LAPACK repaired as for SU(3); the Weyl variant if released.
5. **What the 860 can honestly mean against this literature:** a factor between *our engine's HW step* and *the Munich HW step as distributed* at one point of one family, on two machines; it does not bear on descendants, on other codes, or on the multiplicity basis (which differs: Zarand rref+GS versus Gaudin eigenbasis).

## 3. K2: the outer-multiplicity (missing-label) problem for SU(4)xSU(4) and U(n)

**Verdict: PARTIALLY KNOWN (labels exist, counted, and shown sufficient in general); the specific Gaudin triple NOT FOUND.** Confidence 75%.

### 3.1 How many labels
* Pan-Draayer I `[FT]`: "From this development it is clear that there are at most 3 quantum numbers needed to label the outer multiplicity for the decomposition SU(4) x SU(4) -> SU(4). In the canonical unit tensor approach proposed by Biedenharn et al. for the SU(4) case, there are 4 shifts out of 6 upper labels, of which only 3 labels are independent"; "there is still no general formula for the outer multiplicity of SU(n) with n >= 4" (1998).
* Sharp-Lee, "SU(4) van der Waerden invariant", Rev. Mex. Fis. 20 (1970) 203-215 (`[FT]`, open access at rmf.smf.mx): "the external coupling labels are 1/2 (r + 3l) in number (3l IR labels and 1/2 (r - 3l) 'missing' labels)", r the order of the group, l its rank: for SU(4), r = 15, l = 3, giving 3 missing labels (and 1 for SU(3), 0 for SU(2)); consistent with (N-1)(N-2)/2.

### 3.2 The canonical Biedenharn-Louck scheme
* Louck-Biedenharn, J. Math. Phys. 11 (1970) 2368-2414 (`[AB]`): "A complete, fully explicit, and canonical determination of the matrix elements of all adjoint tensor operators in all U(n) is presented. The class of adjoint tensor operators ... is the first exhibiting a nontrivial multiplicity. It is demonstrated that the canonical resolution of this multiplicity possesses several compatible (or equivalent) properties: classification by null spaces, classification by degree in the Racah invariants, classification by limit properties, and the classification by conjugation parity." Note the last two: labelling by *degree in Gelfand invariants* and by *conjugation parity* are the closest classical ancestors of "labelling by operators of increasing degree" and of the exchange-parity argument of the notes' section 4.3 (for the adjoint, i.e. a different problem: V_lambda x adjoint -> V_lambda).
* Biedenharn-Louck-Chacon-Ciftan (J. Math. Phys. 13 (1972) 1957), Biedenharn-Louck (13 (1972) 1985), Louck-Biedenharn (14 (1973) 1336), Biedenharn-Lohe-Louck (26 (1985) 1458): canonical splitting for U(3) via "maximal null space" of the Wigner operator (`[AB]`). Pan-Draayer (`[FT]`): the canonical unit-tensor method "is particularly useful for deriving multiplicity-free CGCs of U(n) ... but ... has not been used to produce a closed algebraic solution to the general outer multiplicity problem"; their own complementary-group labels give coefficients "orthogonal with respect to the outer multiplicity labels and therefore the scheme that is canonical", they state that "the upper pattern labeling scheme given by Biedenharn et al is also equivalent to our labeling scheme, which will be proved in our next paper", and they remark that the Littlewood-rule restrictions "must apply to the upper pattern labeling scheme, which was not mentioned in [the Biedenharn-Louck] papers, and indeed [are] difficult to be obtained directly from their methods".
* So: a *canonical* (BL) resolution exists for U(3) and for adjoint operators in all U(n); for SU(4) x SU(4) it is formulated (pattern calculus) but not worked out in closed form.

### 3.3 Non-canonical resolutions
Pan-Draayer's survey `[FT]`: Moshinsky; Derome-Sharp; Resnikoff; Pluhar et al.; Hecht; Klimyk-Gavrilik; Le Blanc-Rowe; Alisauskas ("paracanonical coupling relations ... pseudo-canonical coupling schemes"); "noncanonical definitions ... normally lead to non-orthogonality with respect to the outer multiplicity; the Gram-Schmidt process ... includes an arbitrary choice in ordering"; Schur-Weyl routes (Moshinsky, Kramer, Alisauskas-Jucys, Chen et al.: "numerical orthogonalization is required"); for SU(3) the third-order labelling operator of Hecht and the fourth-order operator of Alisauskas-Kulish "a form suggested by Sharp"; "chiral Casimirs". Alishauskas-Kulish, Zap. Nauchn. Sem. LOMI 145 (1985) 3-21 (`[AB]`, "Spectral decomposition of SU(3)-invariant solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation"): a classifying operator built from third- and fourth-degree generator operators; SU(3) only. Mathur-Raychowdhury-Sreeraj (J. Math. Phys. 52 (2011) 113505, arXiv:1108.5246, `[AB]`): SU(N)xSU(N) Schwinger-boson invariants, projection operators in terms of the invariant-group generators, "SU(N) CGCs computed as the matrix elements of the projection operators"; Mathur-Rathor-Sreeraj (arXiv:1906.10410, `[AB]`) for SU(3): the elementary invariants form so(4,2) and give "a family of operators any one of which can be used to distinguish repeating representations". Both are Howe-dual (complementary algebra) constructions.

### 3.4 Polynomial invariants as labels: the theorems (this is the main new finding for K2)
* **Klink-Ton-That, J. Math. Phys. 37 (1996) 6468-6485** (`[AB]`, Crossref): "Decomposing tensor products of irreducible representations of compact groups almost always involves multiplicity ... **We show that the multiplicity can always be specified by polynomial group invariants.** The setting is a Bargmann-Segal-Fock space in n x N complex variables, where n is the number of labels needed to specify the tensor product and N is the dimension of the fundamental representation ... The example of SU(N) is discussed in detail, and it is shown that the multiplicity can be computed as the solution of certain diophantine equations arising from powers of group invariants, namely minors of determinants." Earlier: Klink-Ton-That J. Phys. A 21 (1988) 3877, "On resolving the multiplicity of arbitrary tensor products of the U(N) groups"; J. Funct. Anal. 84 (1989) 1; J. Algebra 145 (1992) 187, "Invariant theory of the block diagonal subgroups of GL(n,C) and generalized Casimir operators"; Klink-Ton-That-Wills, J. Phys. A 26 (1993) 3229-3242, "Shift operators and the U(N) multiplicity problem" (`[AB]`, IOP page: "A computationally effective method for decomposing r-fold tensor products of irreducible representations of U(N) in a basis-independent fashion is given. The multiplicity arising from the tensor decomposition is resolved with the eigenvalues of invariant operators chosen from the universal enveloping algebra generated by the infinitesimal operators of the dual (or complementary) representation. Shift operators which commute with the U(N) invariant operators, but not the dual invariant operators, are introduced to compute the eigenvectors and eigenvalues of the dual invariant operators algebraically. **A three-fold tensor product of irreducible representations of SU(4) is decomposed to illustrate the power and generality of the method.**"); Aulwes-Klink-Ton-That, J. Phys. A 34 (2001) 8237, "Invariant theory, generalized Casimir operators, and tensor product decompositions of U(N)"; Gliske-Klink-Ton-That 2005 (main theorem, above).
* **What a generalized Casimir is** (Gliske thesis section 4, `[FT]`): in the realisation on polynomials in Z (n rows), the generators L_{ij} of the complementary gl(n) are arranged in p_u x p_v blocks [L]_{uv} (p_u = number of rows of the u-th irrep); "operators given as the trace of sums of products of such blocks ... [L]_{i1 i2}[L]_{i2 i3} ... [L]_{id i1}" that are Hermitian "are generalized Casimir operators", they commute with the diagonal algebra; "it has been proven that there exist sufficient generalized Casimir operators to fully break any degeneracy in the product space [Klink-Ton-That 1988]"; "binary coupling method": for each parenthesis the operators C(a,b,c) = tr [L]_{12}([L]_{22})^a [L]_{21} ([L]_{12}[L]_{21})^b ([L]_{11})^c + adjoint with a + 2b + c = d - 2, or the "special" single operator tr [L]_{12}([L]_{22})^{d-2}[L]_{21}, default degree d = N. The author states "Although these methods do not work in general, in practice they are most useful ... To prove that either of these methods is sufficient ... several additional steps would be needed ... While such proofs are yet to be shown, no counter examples have been found to date." For two factors of U(4) the special choice gives *one* operator of degree 4 plus the lower ones; this is consistent with the notes' finding that a single operator does not suffice (3 labels needed).
* **Relation to the Gaudin triple.** These operators live in the commutant of the diagonal gl_N inside the Howe-dual picture: the L_{ij} are complementary-group generators (for two SU(4) irreps n = 2 x 3 = 6, the U(6) of Pan-Draayer), not the e^s_{ij} of the notes. By Howe duality they should generate the same commutant action on multiplicity spaces as the polarised traces T(...) of the notes, and the SU(3) operator tr [L]_{12}[L]_{22}[L]_{21} used in Gliske's example is of the same type as the PST/CPV cubic; but the dictionary between the two presentations is not in any source I read `[INF]`. What the Klink school lacks and the Gaudin construction has: commutativity by construction (Talalaev), the t-independent triple, simple spectrum (Rybnikov 2020), the oper description, the reduction to the slice.

### 3.5 SU(4)-specific labelling
Sharp-Lee 1970 (above, `[FT]`): a symmetric solution of "the SU(4) external labeling problem" "by explicit construction of the general van der Waerden invariant as a product of elementary scalars", with "a one-to-one correspondence between product IR's and internal states" (Speiser's connection): a polynomial-exponent labelling, not an operator one, with the nice property of being symmetric in the three irreps (within phases). Klink-Ton-That-Wills 1993 (SU(4) example); Pan-Draayer (U(6) complementary labels, SU(4) multiplicity formula); Haacke et al. (Baird-Biedenharn); none of them proposes a commuting operator triple of Gaudin type.

### 3.6 Extremal-projector school

### 3.7 Gaudin / Bethe-algebra labels for gl_N
* *General theory* (yesterday, plus today's reading): Vinberg; Rybnikov 2006; FFR 2010; MTV 2009; HKRW 2020; Rybnikov (IMRN, online 2018, arXiv:1608.04625): cyclic vector in the space of singular vectors "for all values of the parameters", hence (with diagonalisability, which for two sites follows from self-adjointness for the invariant form, as in the notes' section 4.3) a simple joint spectrum for two sites too. ## 4. K3: the slice / PRV reduction and the level recursion as an algorithm for U(n)

**Verdict: PARTIALLY KNOWN.** The slice parametrisation of singular vectors is classical and has been used as a *criterion* and as a *definition of intertwiners* in the Verma-module and extremal-projector literature; HW-first constructions exist for U(N); a *numerical level recursion with string and dangling conditions for N >= 4* was not found. Confidence 70% (I could not read Zhelobenko, Gliske et al. 2007, or Tolstoy-Draayer beyond the abstract).

* **The theorem and its attribution** (yesterday): Kostant 1959 (Lemma 4.1), PRV 1967, Kumar (ICM 2010, Theorem 3.7).
* **Slice = "expectation value" of an intertwiner (Etingof-Varchenko 2002, `[FT]`).** For a finite-dimensional (or category-O) module V and generic lambda, "the map Phi -> <Phi> is an isomorphism Hom(M_lambda, M_{lambda-mu} x V) -> V[mu]. In particular, this map is an isomorphism for dominant weights with sufficiently large coordinates (lambda, alpha_i^vee)" (Lemma 1, section 2.5; the authors cite Etingof-Schiffmann(-Varchenko) for the proof); "the matrix elements of the intertwiner ... with respect to the bases induced by any bases in U(n^-) and V are rational functions of lambda". So *the slice/HW-vector correspondence as a function of the Verma highest weight, rational in it, is standard*, and it is the origin of the polynomial dependence on lambda' of the notes' reduced operators. The caveat "generic or sufficiently dominant" matters for the notes' interpretation at small integral lambda' (section 9).
* **Slice + extremal projector as a computational method (Mudrov, J. Pure Appl. Algebra 226 (2022) 106902, arXiv:1911.09933, `[FT]` introduction).** "We employ a parametrization of (V x Z)^+ [the singular vectors] by a certain subspace in one of the tensor factors, e.g. V_{Z+}, isomorphic to Hom_{U_q(g+)}(Z*, V) ... identified with the kernel of the left ideal annihilating the lowest vector in Z"; the pull-back of the contravariant form to this subspace ("extremal twist") is expressed through the *shifted extremal projector* p_g(lambda) (lambda the highest weight of Z), "which equals the inverse element that participated in the construction of the dynamical Weyl group in [EV]"; the result is "a computationally feasible criterion for complete reducibility of tensor products" (quantum groups, classical included). This is the slice in exactly the sense of the notes, with the extremal projector as its universal avatar; it is a criterion, not a CGC engine, and nothing in it resembles the level recursion.
* **HW-first constructions for U(N):** Gliske-Klink-Ton-That (HW vector f_max of the target irrep mapped to the product space; section 2.5); Munich and Dang et al. (null space of the raising operators, which is HW-first but on the whole weight space); Rowe-Repka (shift tensors); Tolstoy-Draayer (projection operators, u(n-1) 9j); Pan-Draayer (reduced Wigner coefficients by recoupling); SU(3) chains (Williams, Braunschweig, Rowe-Bahri; yesterday).
* **Extremal projector school** (`[FT]` Tolstoy review, `[AB]` Khoroshkin-Ogievetsky): Asherova-Smirnov-Tolstoy 1971/1973 (extremal projector, lowering operators for su(n)), the Mickelsson-Zhelobenko algebras (Zhelobenko 1983), reduction algebras and Zhelobenko operators (Khoroshkin-Ogievetsky, J. Algebra 319 (2008) 2113; SIGMA 7 (2011) 064: "We describe, in terms of generators and relations, the reduction algebra related to the diagonal embedding of gl_n into gl_n + gl_n. Its representation theory is related to the theory of decompositions of tensor products of gl_n-modules"). This is the natural algebraic home of "the multiplicity space is a module over a small algebra acting on the slice space" (the notes' "reduced operators"); I did not read these papers for an explicit statement of the reduction rule (9).
* **The level recursion, dangling conditions.** Nothing in the sources above (Alex et al., Dang et al., Gliske, Mudrov, EV, Tolstoy) contains a level-by-level triangular solution of the raising conditions starting from the slice, or the distinction between consistency (T) and dangling (D) conditions; the closest relative in SU(3) is the Williams/Braunschweig chain (yesterday). I found no source on "Zhelobenko's Gauss-decomposition approach to U(n) Wigner coefficients", on Klimyk's U(n) Wigner coefficients beyond the explicit formulas for the defining representation (Vilenkin-Klimyk, `[CIT]` via Alex et al.), or on Alisauskas's recursive U(n) isoscalar factors online (Zhelobenko's book not accessible); Gould's series (J. Math. Phys. 22 (1981) 15, 2376; 27 (1986) 1944) treats *multiplicity-free* reduced Wigner coefficients as rational polynomial functions of Casimir eigenvalues (`[AB]`), so it does not address the multiplicity problem.
* **Verdict for G1:** the statement in the notes, "what we add is its use as an algorithm for gl(4)", is defensible. Cite Mudrov and Etingof-Varchenko for the slice parametrisation, and the Klink school for HW-first algorithms.

### 5(a). Gaudin operators on V[mu - lambda'] for non-dominant lambda': commutation and BGG intertwining (12)

The shifted action w o lambda = w(lambda + rho) - rho is the dot action of the notes. The dynamical Weyl group operator A_{s,V}(lambda): V[mu] -> V[...] "is the effect, at the level of expectation values, of the operation of restriction of an intertwiner from M_lambda to M_{s o lambda}" (section 3.1); Lemma 4: "the operation of fusion of intertwiners commutes with the operation of restriction of intertwiners to submodules". The dynamical Weyl group "may be regarded as a generalisation of the classical extremal projectors [AST]; the operators for simple Lie algebras were introduced in [Zhelobenko]". For sl_2 and the vector representation, A_V(lambda) v_+ = q v_-, A_V(lambda) v_- = -q^{-1} [lambda+2]/[lambda+1] v_+, and in general A^k_m(lambda) is, up to a constant, the product of k ratios [lambda+1+j]/[lambda-m+k+j] (Proposition 6; classical limit q -> 1 gives rational functions). Styrkas-Tarasov-Varchenko (Adv. * *Commutation on the whole weight space for every lambda' (notes section 5.4, item 2).* `[INF]` This follows from known results by a Zariski-density argument: for fixed weight kappa, the operators V_red(lambda')|V[kappa] depend polynomially on lambda'; for generic lambda' (M(lambda'), M(lambda' + kappa) simple Verma modules) the slice map Hom(M(lambda'+kappa), V x M(lambda')) -> V[kappa] is an isomorphism (EV Lemma 1), the reduction rule of the notes' section 5.1 makes O -> Red(O) an algebra homomorphism on all of V[kappa], and the Gaudin algebra is commutative (Talalaev); hence the commutators of the V_red vanish for generic lambda' and therefore identically. The notes list this as verified, not proved; with this argument it is a corollary.
* *BGG intertwining (12).* Not found. `[INF]` In category-O terms it should be the transpose, with respect to the invariant forms, of the A-equivariant injections Hom(M(mu), V x M(s_i o lambda')) -> Hom(M(mu), V x M(lambda')) induced by the Verma embedding M(s_i o lambda') -> M(lambda'); those injections have zero slice (their images lie in V x n^- M(lambda')), which is why the intertwiner in slice coordinates points the other way and is E_i^{k_i}, and why M_mu = the common kernel of the E_i^{k_i}. This also explains item 3 of the notes' section 5.4 (M_mu a spectral subspace, the w - m other eigenvectors violate a string): see 5(b).

### 5(b). The Weyl/BGG identity (18) for the physical spectrum

**Verdict: NOT FOUND in print; provable from category O `[INF]`.** Searches: four web queries combining {Euler characteristic, BGG resolution, Weyl group, dot action, Kostant multiplicity} with {Bethe, Gaudin, joint spectrum, Verma}, and arXiv-API queries ("Gaudin" + "Verma module": three unrelated hits, cyclotomic Gaudin models and higher Sugawara operators; "Bethe algebra" + BGG / Weyl group: none; "Gaudin" + Kostant: one unrelated hit; "Gaudin" + "multiplicity space": only Halacheva-Kamnitzer-Rybnikov-Weekes); nothing. Kostant's multiplicity formula is the dimension shadow (notes, end of section 7.2); the Frenkel passage above is the nearest relative.

**Oper-side counterpart (known in outline).** The oper sees only the *unordered* sets of exponents at its singular points (the notes' section 6.2: indicial polynomials prod_c (e - (nu_c + 4 - c))), so the same Fuchsian operator, hence the same joint eigenvalue, is compatible with every dot-translate of the weight at any one of the three points. This is Frenkel's Weyl-twisted exponents (section 5 of math/0407524, `[FT]`, above) and, on the Bethe-ansatz side, the Mukhin-Varchenko populations: the points of a population (Miura reductions of one oper, i.e. full flags in the same space of polynomials) are related by the reproduction procedure, which changes the weight of the Bethe vector by the dot action; "a population is isomorphic to the flag variety of the Langlands dual Lie algebra" (arXiv:math/0312406, `[AB]`), "the Weyl group of g acts on the points of a population freely and transitively (under certain conditions)" (arXiv:math/0604048, trigonometric Gaudin, `[AB]`); the statement that reproduction shifts the weight of the Bethe vector by the dot action is from memory `[MEM]`, UNVERIFIED. So 'the same eigenvalue occurs at the translate weights, for Verma modules' is known; the *signed count on weight spaces of the finite-dimensional factor that isolates the physical part* (18) is what I did not find.

**Proof sketch `[INF]` (my own, about 30 minutes of thought, not checked by computer; please verify before relying on it).** Notation: mu dominant integral (the case of the notes), lambda' dominant integral, V = V_lambda, A = the Gaudin algebra acting on V x M(nu) for every nu, g-modules in category O.
1. *Projectivity.* For mu dominant integral, M(mu) is projective in O (equivalently P(mu) = M(mu): by BGG reciprocity [P(mu): M(xi)] = [M(xi): L(mu)], and L(mu) is a composition factor of M(xi) only if mu <= xi and xi in W o mu; mu being the maximal element of its dot-orbit, xi = mu; Humphreys, *Representations of semisimple Lie algebras in the BGG category O*, section 3 `[MEM]`, UNVERIFIED theorem numbers). Hence Hom_g(M(mu), -) is exact on O. This is the point where the notes' section 7.2 says "(only left exact)".
2. *Complex.* The BGG resolution 0 -> M(w_0 o lambda') -> ... -> M(lambda') -> L(lambda') -> 0 stays exact after tensoring with V (vector-space tensor), and after applying Hom_g(M(mu), -). The Gaudin algebra A (elements of U(g) x U(g)) acts on V x M(nu) for every nu, commutes with the diagonal g, and commutes with 1 x phi for every g-map phi: M(nu') -> M(nu). So one obtains an exact complex of finite-dimensional A-modules with terms SV(w) = Hom_g(M(mu), V x M(w o lambda')) and last term Hom_g(M(mu), V x L(lambda')) = the multiplicity space M_mu.
3. *Dimensions.* dim SV(nu) = [V x M(nu): L(mu)] = sum_kappa dim V[kappa] [M(nu + kappa): L(mu)] (Verma flag of V x M(nu) and projectivity), and by the argument in step 1 only kappa = mu - nu contributes with multiplicity 1: dim SV(nu) = dim V[mu - nu] for every nu (not only generic), which also reproduces Kostant's formula.
4. *Identification of the A-action.* Deform (mu, nu) -> (mu + eps, nu + eps) with the weight kappa = mu - nu fixed. For generic eps the slice map is an A-equivariant isomorphism SV -> V[kappa] intertwining A with Red(nu + eps) (EV Lemma 1 and the reduction rule of notes section 5.1); at eps = 0 the dimension of the kernel space is the same (step 3), so the spaces SV form a vector bundle of constant rank across eps = 0 and A acts on it algebraically; characteristic polynomials of A on SV agree with those of V_red(nu + eps) on V[kappa] for eps != 0, hence at eps = 0. (The slice map itself fails to be an isomorphism at eps = 0: see 9.)
5. *Conclusion.* Characteristic polynomials (joint characteristic polynomials of a commuting family) are multiplicative on exact sequences, so the alternating product over w of the joint characteristic polynomials of V_red(w o lambda') on V[mu - w o lambda'] equals that of the physical space M_mu: this is (18) as an identity of joint spectral multiplicities. The same picture explains: the filtration SV(e) = Hom(M(mu), V x M(lambda')) with kernel of the slice map = sum of the images of SV(s_i) and quotient M_mu (so M_mu is a spectral subspace of W and the w - m other eigenvalues are those of the translate spaces: the "S(tau) = T(tau)" observation), and the one-/two-string inclusion-exclusion of the notes (81 triples violating two strings lie in four translate spaces {e, s_i, s_j, w_ij}) is the BGG complex in length 2.
* What remains unproved even then: step 4 in detail; the stronger claims "S = T" and "no translate of length >= 3 needed" as stated for individual eigenvectors (the K_0 argument gives multiplicities, not the pointwise statement); sectors with mu not dominant do not occur in the notes. A hand check of (12) and (18) in the simplest case (sl_2, quadratic Gaudin operator Omega = e (x) f + f (x) e + h (x) h / 2; `[INF]`): by the reduction rule Red(Omega)(b) on V[kappa] equals -f e + b kappa / 2, i.e. -(a(a+2) - kappa(kappa+2))/4 + b kappa / 2 on V = V(a), and a direct computation (also checked symbolically with sympy in the scratchpad) gives Red(Omega)(b)|V[kappa] = Red(Omega)(-b-2)|V[kappa + 2b + 2] identically in a, b, kappa; at kappa = mu - b the same formula equals the true eigenvalue [mu(mu+2) - a(a+2) - b(b+2)]/4 of Omega on V(mu) in V(a) x V(b). ### 5(c). The forced oper dictionary, polynomial solutions of degrees mu_j + N - j, the Wronskian, completeness, direct/dual = adjoint + gauge

**Verdict: KNOWN (dictionary, exponents, degrees, completeness, simple spectrum); PARTIALLY KNOWN (adjoint + gauge); explicit gl(4) Table 3 not found (bookkeeping).** Confidence 85%.

D_{N+1} be a factorization into linear factors, D_i = d + f_i, then D^dagger = D_{N+1} ... D_1" (the daggers/stars on the factors were lost in my text extraction; I read the statement as the reversed product, i.e. the formal adjoint up to signs and gauge). ### 5(e). CG components polynomial in the labelling eigenvalues

**Verdict: NOT FOUND for N >= 4; elementary per sector `[INF]`; the uniform versions are known for sl_2 and implicit for sl_3.**
* *Per sector.* Given m distinct joint eigenvalues tau_j in C^3 and eigenvectors v_j, any function tau_j -> (v_j)_k on the m points is a polynomial in the triple; the minimal degree is the Hilbert-function degree of the point set, equal to the generic counting minimum d_gen(m) = min{d: C(d+3,3) >= m} when the points impose independent conditions on polynomials of degree d_gen. The notes' observation (162 of 181 sectors attain d_gen, 19 need d_gen + 1, never less) is therefore a statement about the *position of the joint spectrum* (general position), not about CG coefficients as such; the existence of a cyclic coordinate (Rybnikov 2020, cyclic vector) is what makes the polynomials well defined. Unlike the one-operator case (degree m - 1, adjugate columns), no source states the multivariate counting.
* *Uniform versions.* sl_2: CG coefficients are dual Hahn / Hahn polynomials (Koornwinder, Nieuw Arch. Wisk. (3) 29 (1981) 140-155, `[CIT]`; Nikiforov-Suslov-Uvarov; Granovskii-Zhedanov 1988, `[CIT]` via CPV; Scarabotti, Methods Appl. Anal. 14 (2007) 355-386, multidimensional Hahn polynomials and CG coefficients, `[AB]`; Crampe-Poulain d'Andecy-Vinet SIGMA 22 (2026) 060, `[AB]`: "It is well known that the CG coefficients of sl_2 are given in terms of the dual Hahn polynomials. Taking the reversed point of view, any finite-dimensional family of polynomials belonging to the Askey scheme can be interpreted as CG coefficients of an algebra"). sl_3: CPV's tridiagonal X_m (yesterday). N >= 4: nothing.

### 5(f). The oper as the h -> 0 limit of an XXX Baxter equation

**Verdict: KNOWN.** Confidence 90%.
* MTV, J. Stat. Mech. (2006) P08002, arXiv:math/0605015 (`[FT]` introduction and Appendix A): "The Gaudin model can be obtained from the XXX-type model in the limit as u, z tend to infinity. In this limit the difference operators D^{N,Q}(u; e^{-d_u}; z) and the eigenvalue operators turn into the differential operators D^K(u; d_u; z) ...: D^{N,Q}(eps^{-1}u, ...; eps^{-1}z) = eps^N D^K + O(eps^{N+1}) as Q = 1 + K eps and eps -> 0. Moreover, the Bethe ansatz equations of the XXX-type model become in this limit the Bethe ansatz equations of the Gaudin model, and the Bethe vector turns into the Bethe vector of the Gaudin model" (Appendix A, "The Gaudin model as a limit of the XXX-type model", Theorem A.1); the approach "that gives the transfer matrices of the Gaudin model as a suitable limit of proper linear combinations of the transfer matrices of the XXX-type model" is Talalaev's [T]. Talalaev (hep-th/0404153, `[FT]`): the Bethe subalgebra of Y(gl_n) with the rescaling T(u) = 1 + h E/u and the limit h -> 0 gives the quantum Gaudin Hamiltonians QI_k(u). Ilin-Rybnikov (Transformation Groups 26 (2021) 537, arXiv:2009.06934, `[AB]`): the associated graded of Bethe subalgebras of Y(g) is the universal Gaudin subalgebra of U(g[t]) at the critical level, "generalizing Talalaev's formula". Chervov-Talalaev, hep-th/0604128 (`[FT]`): quantum characteristic polynomial for XXX and Gaudin, the latter as a degeneration. These cover the gl_N statement the brief asks for; the cleanest single citation is MTV 2006, Appendix A.
